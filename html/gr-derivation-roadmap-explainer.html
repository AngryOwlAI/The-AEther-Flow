Summary of GR Derivation Roadmap

The GR derivation roadmap is the project's control surface for the open physics burden. It keeps the public target conservative: ordinary GR remains the observable benchmark, while a first-principles derivation from AEther or AEther-flow substrate structure remains unresolved. The roadmap names the physics objects that must exist before benchmark promotion can be considered, records their current status in the Distance-to-GR ledger, and requires future physics AgentJobs to declare the milestone and burden they are trying to advance. This prevents a useful candidate, a workflow completion, or a generated explanation from being mistaken for a completed derivation. It also gives negative results a stable place: repeated selector underdetermination, scoped obstruction, source-extension pressure, or finite toy-model failure can freeze a route without rejecting the entire ontology.

Milestone Chain

research_control/design/gr_derivation_burden_map.mdregistries/DISTANCE_TO_GR_LEDGER.csv

The milestone chain separates the physics objects that must be built from the workflow role that builds them. The current path begins with source ontology primitives and source equivalence, then moves through `ObsLoc_lc`, `Resp_lc`, `M_src`, `g_eff`, matter coupling, Einstein equations, benchmark promotion, and a finite toy metric-response target. Some objects are draft or witnessed; others are not started or blocked by missing primitives. This ordering matters because a later object cannot be accepted merely because an earlier control task was completed. Each row names a required object, current status, blocking burden, accept criteria, and failure or freeze criteria.

Distance-to-GR Ledger

research_control/design/gr_derivation_burden_map.mdregistries/DISTANCE_TO_GR_LEDGER.csvresearch_control/README.md

The Distance-to-GR ledger is the persistent status matrix for the same burdens. It records whether a row is not started, draft, witnessed, stress-passed, human-gated, accepted, frozen negative, or blocked by a missing primitive. Future physics completions must update the expanded burden matrix and include at least one new mathematical payload. The ledger is therefore a discipline of accounting, not a proof by bookkeeping. It helps agents avoid losing track of partial progress while preserving the difference between a local row status and acceptance of the full derivation route.

Selector And Freeze Criteria

The theoretical-continuation selector exists because missing local data is not automatically a terminal result in a theoretical physics project. When the next honest step is a choice among selector primitives, concrete witnesses, bounded calculations, source-extension audits, or scoped no-go questions, the Director routes one bounded selector job instead of stopping generically. If the same burden recurs, the completion must evaluate freeze criteria. The current response-localization route names `NDCL-RESP-LC-SELECTOR-UNDERDETERMINATION` as a candidate freeze label. That label freezes a specific underdetermined line; it does not prove that every future ontology extension or source-side construction is impossible.

Source Extension And Finite Toy Target

research_control/design/gr_derivation_burden_map.mdAGENTS.md

Source extension is a controlled category, not an informal escape hatch. A proposed extension must be classified as derivation from current ontology, conservative definitional extension, new ontology primitive, or forbidden target-GR import. The finite toy metric-response target provides a constructive intermediate test before the project attempts a full source-to-GR derivation. It asks for a finite source set, local readout syntax, orientation and normalization candidates, an induced response relation, a toy metric analogue, and invariance checks. Passing such a toy target would still be draft scaffolding; it cannot establish `g_eff`, coupling, field equations, or benchmark recovery by itself.

Claim Boundary

AGENTS.mdregistries/CLAIM_BOUNDARY_REGISTRY.csvresearch_control/design/gr_derivation_burden_map.md

The roadmap can report planning state, burden state, permitted next packets, freeze labels, and ledger updates. It cannot adopt ontology, derive the effective metric, prove universal matter coupling, derive the Einstein equations, promote the benchmark, or issue a Gate Chair decision. This boundary is the practical value of the roadmap. It lets the project continue disciplined theoretical construction while preserving the exact difference between a local calculation, a route freeze, a source-extension question, and a physics claim that would require the full authority chain.
