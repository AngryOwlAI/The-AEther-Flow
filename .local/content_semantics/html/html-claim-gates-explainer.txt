Summary of Claim Gates
Claim gates are the project’s control mechanism for deciding when a physics statement may move from framing, proposal, repair, audit, or explanation into stronger accepted status. Their function is to keep exact-GR benchmark adoption separate from unproven substrate derivation claims by requiring source evidence, explicit claim-boundary records, routed review, Gate Chair or human-gated authority when needed, and registry updates before promotion. They matter because polished explainers, completed tasks, and preserved repair packets can make candidate ideas look more settled than they are. A visual explanation, validator pass, or completed AgentJob cannot authorize science claims by itself. Claim gates also protect negative results by preserving why a route is blocked, refuted, underdetermined, or still conjectural.

Status Ladder
registries/CLAIM_BOUNDARY_REGISTRY.csv
README.md
The status ladder separates ontology framing, exact-GR benchmark adoption, candidate derivation work, audit findings, repair attempts, refutations, blocked promotion, negative results, and accepted claims. The ladder matters because each status has different evidentiary weight. A candidate can be useful without being accepted. A repair can reduce one defect without closing the derivation. A refutation can become durable memory rather than failure to hide. Accepted status requires the right gate and source evidence, not only successful prose or a completed workflow.

Gate review is different from ordinary validation. Validators can check file structure, source hashes, registry consistency, task boundaries, and required receipts. They cannot decide that a speculative physics claim is now accepted. A promotion request needs a defined claim, source evidence, audit history, allowed role authority, and a gate capable of evaluating scientific status. The Gate Chair role is status-defined and human-gated; ordinary agents cannot silently exercise it because the consequences include scientific promotion or rejection.
Negative Results

registries/RESEARCH_TASK_REGISTRY.csv
registries/CLAIM_BOUNDARY_REGISTRY.csv
Negative results are preserved because they prevent the project from repeating the same failed path under a new label. A smuggling audit, obstruction, blocked derivation, failed repair, or underdetermination result can be valuable if it records what was tested, which source assumptions were used, why the route failed, and what future work should avoid. The research-control chain turns that result into task artifacts, registry rows, handoff state, and claim boundaries, so non-success remains research memory rather than disappearing from the project narrative.
Forbidden Promotion
AGENTS.md
registries/CLAIM_BOUNDARY_REGISTRY.csv
Forbidden promotion occurs when a statement gains stronger status without the right authority path. Examples include treating generated HTML as science authority, treating workflow completion as proof, treating exact-GR adoption as substrate derivation, or treating a local calculation as a general theorem. The boundary is practical: it keeps language honest while still allowing bold candidate construction. A page may say what was attempted, what passed locally, and what remains blocked; it may not turn those records into accepted physics.
