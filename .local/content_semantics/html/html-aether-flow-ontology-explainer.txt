Summary of Æther-flow Ontology
The Æther-flow ontology is the project’s vocabulary for a proposed deeper substrate, its ordered motion, and the observer-level world that appears as space, time-order, expansion, gravity, matter behavior, and relativistic geometry. Its function is conceptual and methodological: it gives candidate construction something precise to talk about while keeping exact general relativity as the observable benchmark. The ontology does not by itself prove a replacement for GR. The current research burden is to recover Lorentzian metric structure, causal behavior, clock behavior, same-metric matter coupling, invariance, and closure from source-side substrate data without importing the target geometry by hand. That makes anti-smuggling discipline part of the ontology’s function. The vocabulary matters because the project is trying to distinguish an interpretive picture, a mathematical model, and an accepted empirical theory rather than letting those categories collapse.

In ordinary language, the ontology is the project’s proposed picture of what may exist beneath the equations used by relativity. Æther names the deeper substrate; Æther-flow names ordered motion in that substrate; observed space and S-time name the level at which observers experience geometry, clocks, expansion, and gravity. The point is not to abandon exact GR where it works. The point is to ask whether the familiar geometry can be recovered from a more primitive source description. That makes the ontology a disciplined vocabulary for inquiry, not a completed physical theory.
Project Terms

The key terms are functional. Æther is the proposed four-dimensional substrate. Æther-flow is the intrinsic ordered motion that the project uses to talk about experienced order, apparent spatial structure, and gravitational organization. Observed three-dimensional space is the local experiential slice, not necessarily the whole source structure. S-time is the experienced order of change, not a free permission to redefine physics. Adoption means using exact GR as the benchmark model; derivation means recovering that model from source-side assumptions. Anti-smuggling means the target GR structure cannot be inserted by assumption and then advertised as recovered.
Benchmark Boundary

The exact-GR benchmark is the project’s guardrail. Current valid calculations may use ordinary GR as the observable target, but a first-principles Æther-flow derivation would need to explain why that target emerges. A successful derivation would have to produce effective Lorentzian geometry, causal cones, clock behavior, matter coupling to the same metric, invariance properties, and Einsteinian closure from source-defined data. Until that burden is met and gated, the ontology can frame research but cannot promote itself to accepted physics.
Derivation Burden

The burden is not a slogan; it is a sequence of missing mathematical obligations. The source side must define substrate data, a valid observer readout, an effective metric, causal and clock behavior, universal same-metric matter coupling, control of nonmetric modes, S-time closure, and invariance without importing GR by hand. Each candidate packet can satisfy a local obligation, fail, or reveal an obstruction. The claim-gate system preserves those outcomes so partial progress is not inflated into a completed derivation.

Claim Boundary

The ontology may explain a proposed substrate interpretation, candidate vocabulary, and open derivation burden. It may not assert that GR has been derived from Æther-flow, that a new empirical prediction has been established, that canonical TeX has changed, or that a generated page has authority. The boundary protects both creativity and rigor. It allows speculative model-building while requiring every promotion to pass through source evidence, role authority, validators, and gates. Operationally, that means every reader should ask whether a statement is vocabulary, candidate mathematics, benchmark adoption, audit result, or accepted physics before carrying it into another document.
