# EqSrc RetainH GenH Trigger List v16

## Purpose

This note defines when future continue-research packets must route upstream
primitive work for `EqSrc`, `RetainH`, or `GenH`.

The policy is a routing trigger list only. It does not discharge general
`EqSrc`, adopt `RetainH`, adopt `GenH`, adopt a source law, derive matter
coupling, promote a benchmark, authorize Gate Chair closure, or complete the
derivation.

## Current Status Basis

The tracked Distance-to-GR ledger and frontier inventory classify:

- `EqSrc` as a draft/control object whose general equivalence theorem remains
  missing.
- `RetainH` as blocked by a missing retention primitive or theorem.
- `GenH` as blocked by a missing generator primitive or theorem.

The v15 upstream-dependency audit established the operative routing boundary:
these primitives are not required for the current explicitly certificated
matter-sector theorem scope, but they become conditionally required when a
future task broadens beyond explicit certificate records or uses
`H`-indexed family behavior as theorem input.

## Trigger Principle

Route an upstream primitive packet only when a future task's stated theorem,
construction, selector, or proof obligation actually depends on that primitive.
Do not route `EqSrc`, `RetainH`, or `GenH` merely because the primitive is
interesting, adjacent, elegant, philosophically appealing, or listed as an open
frontier burden.

The selector must ask two questions:

1. Does the future task remove a protection that explicit certificate records
   currently supply?
2. Does the future task make a theorem or construction claim that cannot be
   checked without a general source-equivalence, retention, or generator
   primitive?

If both answers are no, the upstream primitive remains horizon-only.

## Exact Trigger Rules

### Route EqSrc

Route an `EqSrc` packet when a future task:

- removes explicit certificate premises;
- seeks family-wide source equivalence;
- needs equivalence across arbitrary source-family variation;
- claims certificate equivalence independent of certificate records.

The routed packet must name the specific equivalence domain, variation regime,
objects related, and failure mode if the general equivalence theorem is absent.

### Route RetainH

Route a `RetainH` packet when a future task:

- preserves certificate structure under `H`;
- preserves source semantics under retention;
- claims stability under an `H`-indexed operation;
- needs retention to sustain matter-sector continuity.

The routed packet must name the retained structure, the `H`-indexed operation,
the invariant or preservation claim, and the boundary between local witness
survival and a general retention law.

### Route GenH

Route a `GenH` packet when a future task:

- constructs an `H`-indexed source family;
- enumerates a generated source family;
- requires generator closure;
- uses generated family structure as theorem input.

The routed packet must name the generator, generated family, closure condition,
and theorem input that depends on generated-family structure.

## Allowed Non-Trigger Cases

Do not route `EqSrc`, `RetainH`, or `GenH` when a task:

- uses explicit certificate records and does not claim record-independent
  equivalence;
- checks a finite local certificate instance without generalizing to arbitrary
  source-family variation;
- records a negative fixture, malformed certificate, or fail-closed branch;
- refreshes generated wiki, graph, registry, or current-frontier derivatives;
- improves project-control validators, schemas, renderers, or documentation;
- cites `EqSrc`, `RetainH`, or `GenH` only as blocked upstream context;
- uses local witness survival without claiming `RetainH`;
- enumerates examples without claiming `GenH` closure;
- performs selector ranking without selecting an upstream primitive route.

These cases may mention upstream primitive status, but they must keep the
status as horizon context unless a trigger rule above is satisfied.

## Forbidden Overread

The following interpretations are blocked:

- Treating this trigger list as a proof of general `EqSrc`.
- Treating a routed packet as `RetainH` or `GenH` adoption.
- Treating a local or finite witness as a general equivalence, retention, or
  generator theorem.
- Treating source-extension data as canonical ontology.
- Treating validator PASS, generated derivative freshness, or checkpoint
  success as physics evidence.
- Treating "not triggered" as a no-go theorem against future conservative
  source-side extension.
- Treating a human-gate authorization for one packet as downstream matter
  coupling, benchmark, or completed-derivation authority.

## Completion Fragment

Future selector and theorem-setup completions should include:

```yaml
upstream_primitive_trigger_status:
  EqSrc:
    triggered: false
    reason: "explicit certificate records retained; no record-independent equivalence claim"
  RetainH:
    triggered: false
    reason: "no H-indexed retention claim"
  GenH:
    triggered: false
    reason: "no generated-family closure or theorem-input claim"
```

When a primitive is triggered, `reason` must cite the exact trigger rule and
the source artifact or task section that makes the primitive necessary.

## Selector Use

For future selector packets:

1. Evaluate the selected theorem or construction against the three trigger
   sections.
2. Record one status block for each primitive.
3. If any primitive is triggered, route exactly one bounded upstream primitive
   packet unless the task explicitly authorizes a combined packet.
4. If none are triggered, keep the current selected theorem lane and record
   the non-trigger rationale.
5. Preserve all downstream claim blocks.

## Distance-to-GR Effect

This policy changes routing discipline only. It does not update the
Distance-to-GR ledger, discharge a burden, or alter source-object status.

## Acceptance Check

The trigger list is exact, includes non-trigger cases, includes forbidden
overread language, and creates no physics delta. It routes P9-T02 to integrate
the trigger-status block into selector packets and DAG edge ranking.
