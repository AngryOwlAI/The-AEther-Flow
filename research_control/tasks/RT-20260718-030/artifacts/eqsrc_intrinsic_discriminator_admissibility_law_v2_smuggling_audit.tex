For a linear self-map \(L:A_E\to A_E\), relation preservation is equivalent
to \(L(B_E)\subseteq B_E\). When \(L\) is invertible, it is a relation
automorphism exactly when \(L(B_E)=B_E\). These claims pass.

\begin{theorem}[Invertible-linear mismatch count]
Let \(A=\mathbb F_q^n\), let \(B\leq A\) have dimension \(k\), and let
\(L\in\mathrm{GL}(A)\). Put
\[
r=\dim(B\cap L^{-1}B).
\]
The number of ordered pairs \((x,y)\in A^2\) for which membership of
\(x-y\) in \(B\) differs from membership of \(Lx-Ly\) in \(B\) is
\[
2q^n(q^k-q^r).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each difference \(d=x-y\), exactly \(q^n\) ordered pairs realize \(d\).
The mismatch differences form the symmetric difference
\(B\triangle L^{-1}B\). Both subspaces have \(q^k\) elements and their
intersection has \(q^r\) elements, so the symmetric difference has
\(2(q^k-q^r)\) elements.
\end{proof}

\section{Chain-Map Reflection Obstruction}

For a typed chain map \(F:E\to E'\), the chain condition maps cycles to
cycles and boundaries into boundaries. It induces
\[
H_1(F):K_E\to K_{E'}
\]
and obeys
\[
\chi_{E'}F_1=H_1(F)\chi_E.
\]
Thus \(F_1\) preserves the cross-complex relation. It reflects the relation
if and only if \(H_1(F)\) is injective. A chain isomorphism is sufficient, but
whole-chain invertibility is not necessary.

\begin{proposition}[Noninvertible reflecting chain map]
Let
\[
C_2=\Ftwo\langle f\rangle,\quad
C_1=\Ftwo\langle a,b\rangle,\quad C_0=0,\qquad
\partial_2f=a.
\]
Define a self-chain-map by
\[
F_2=0,\qquad F_1(a)=0,\qquad F_1(b)=b,\qquad F_0=0.
\]
Then \(F_1\) is noninvertible, while \(H_1(F)\) is the identity on
\(C_1/\langle a\rangle\). Consequently \(F_1\) preserves and reflects the
kernel-pair relation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The chain condition holds because
\(F_1\partial_2f=F_1(a)=0=\partial_2F_2f\).
The quotient has basis \([b]\), and \(H_1(F)[b]=[b]\). Hence the induced map is
the identity and is injective. Direct enumeration of the four source states
confirms zero mismatches over all \(16\) ordered pairs.
\end{proof}

The candidate's quotient-factorization theorem contains this exact
injectivity criterion. Its fail-closed item 5 nevertheless rejects relation
reflection whenever a chain map is noninvertible. That rule is false and
internally inconsistent with the preceding theorem.
