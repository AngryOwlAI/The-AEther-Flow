\section{Nonfaithful actions}

Let \(\rho:G\to\operatorname{Sym}(D)\) be an action and
\(N=\ker\rho\). The action factors through the faithful image
\(\bar\rho:G/N\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sym}(D)\).

\begin{proposition}[Faithful-quotient fixed-locus invariance]
\[
  D^G=D^{G/N}=D^{\rho(G)}.
\]
Consequently, faithfulness is not an assumption needed by the fixed-point
classification or its empty-fixed-locus obstruction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A point is fixed by every element of \(G\) exactly when it is fixed by every
permutation in \(\rho(G)\). Elements in the same coset of \(N\) induce the
same permutation, so this is exactly fixedness under \(G/N\).
\end{proof}

The minimal finite control uses the Klein four group on \(D=\{0,1\}\):
one generator swaps the two points and the other acts trivially. The kernel
has order two, both the full and faithful-quotient fixed loci are empty, and
the deterministic theorem is unchanged.

\section{Multiple components}

For component fixed loci \(F_i\), P2-T03 already proves
\(\Sel(\G)\cong\prod_iF_i\). Two bounded profiles expose the essential
guards:
\[
  |F_1|=2,\ |F_2|=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\Sel(\G)|=0,
\]
while
\[
  |F_1|=2,\ |F_2|=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad|\Sel(\G)|=2.
\]
Thus one multiple fixed locus does not imply global multiplicity if another
factor is empty. For an arbitrary infinite component family, factorwise
nonemptiness still does not supply a selector without an actual product
element or a declared suitable choice principle. The multiple-component
stress therefore confirms rather than weakens the stated theorem.

\section{Stochastic selector boundary}

A probability-valued selector takes values in the simplex \(\Delta(D)\),
not in \(D\). Its invariant locus can be nonempty when \(D^G\) is empty.

\begin{countermodel}[Transitive three-point stochastic boundary]
Let \(C_3\) act transitively on \(D=\{0,1,2\}\) by cyclic rotation. Then
\(D^{C_3}=\varnothing\), but
\[
  \mu=\tfrac13(\delta_0+\delta_1+\delta_2)
\]
is fixed by the induced action on \(\Delta(D)\).
\end{countermodel}

This is a minimal countermodel to the overread ``no invariant point implies
no invariant randomized selector.'' It is not a countermodel to P2-T03,
which explicitly classifies deterministic point-valued natural sections and
disclaims randomized-selection conclusions. Orbit averaging also illustrates
the collapse branch: distinct point choices collapse to one invariant
distribution without selecting an invariant point.

\section{Partial morphism boundary}

A partial function \(p:D\rightharpoonup D\) is not a morphism in
\(\mathbf{Set}\) unless it is total. A partial swap with domain \(\{0\}\)
and \(p(0)=1\) on \(D=\{0,1\}\) has neither a total Set-map interpretation
nor a groupoid inverse. It therefore cannot be inserted into the declared
functor \(S_\chi:\G\to\mathbf{Set}\) as though the existing naturality
equations applied unchanged.

A partial-action extension would need a separately defined category or
restriction structure, domains of definition, composition compatibility,
inverse or restriction axioms, and a partial naturality or cocycle law. The
absence of those data is a concrete inverse-and-cocycle applicability gap,
not a refutation of the total-groupoid theorem. No such law is constructed or
adopted here.

\section{Added marks}

On an unmarked two-point swap action, the fixed locus is empty. Add a
source-side mark naming point \(0\), require automorphisms to preserve it,
and restrict eligibility to the marked singleton. The new marked problem has
the unique fixed choice \(0\). This does not contradict the unmarked result:
the object signature, automorphism group, or eligible-choice functor has
changed.

A target-neutral mark can be legitimate proposal-only source-extension data,
but the original theorem cannot derive its availability or relevance. If the
mark directly records the desired choice, the selection burden has been
encoded in the new input rather than solved. Adoption remains human-gated.

\section{Altered variation classes}

\begin{proposition}[Variation-subgroup monotonicity]
If \(H\leq G\) acts on \(D\) by restriction, then
\[
  D^G\subseteq D^H.
\]
The inclusion may be strict.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every point fixed by all elements of \(G\) is fixed by the subset \(H\).
For the swap action on two points, \(D^G=\varnothing\), whereas restriction
to the identity subgroup gives \(D^H=D\).
\end{proof}

Narrowing the admitted variation class can create selectors or multiplicity;
widening it can remove them. The theorem correctly reports the result for the
declared class, but does not derive which transformations current ontology or
physics must identify. This is the finite-variation-fragility branch and the
central remaining source-law burden.
