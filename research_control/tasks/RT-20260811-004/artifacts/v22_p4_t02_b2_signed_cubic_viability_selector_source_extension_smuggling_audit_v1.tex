\section{Audit standard}

``Source-pure as written'' means only that the disclosed mathematical premises
are source-typed and do not secretly use target geometry, desired empirical
behavior, frozen outputs, or workflow status as scientific premises.  It does
not mean that the premises are derived, natural, epistemically preferred,
robust, adopted, physical, empirical, universal, or adequate for P4--T02.

A desired property may be constructively preloaded without being hidden.  In
that case the local source-purity test can pass while the result earns no
derivational credit.  That is the situation here: $\sigma$ explicitly names
one of two otherwise symmetric source components.

\section{Exact new audit payloads}

\subsection{Dimensionless reduction}

Set
\[
 x=\frac{a}{a_\star},\qquad
 \tau=\frac{t}{\Delta},\qquad
 H=\gamma\Delta a_\star^2.
\]
Then
\[
 x(\tau)=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+2H\tau x^2}},\qquad
 b_\sigma=\frac{\sigma x}{\sqrt{1+x^2}},\qquad
 \lambda=(1+2H)^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus the quantitative bounded-step certificate has one continuous
dimensionless parameter $H$ plus the separate discrete token $\sigma$.
Under
\[
 a'=da,\quad a_\star'=da_\star,\quad t'=ct,\quad
 \Delta'=c\Delta,\quad \gamma'=\frac{\gamma}{cd^2},
\]
$x$, $\tau$, $H$, $b_\sigma$, and $\lambda$ are invariant.  Moreover, $H$ is
complete for this positive-regraduation action.  If two positive triples have
the same $H$, choosing $c=\Delta'/\Delta$ and
$d=a_\star'/a_\star$ transports one to the other; choosing
$c=1/\Delta$ and $d=1/a_\star$ gives the unique normal form $(H,1,1)$.
This is exact
source-coordinate covariance.  It is not a physical gauge statement or a
derived unit system.

\subsection{Parameter roles and component selection}

For every $\gamma>0$, $\Delta>0$, and $a_\star>0$, the flow preserves the
sign of every nonzero finite-time state.  Therefore the equality
$\Viab_{R_\Delta}(U_\sigma)=U_\sigma$ is independent of the numerical values
of those three continuous parameters.  They change the rate and the
certificate factor; they do not choose between $U_+$ and $U_-$.  The entire
component-identity choice is carried by the supplied token $\sigma$.

More precisely, the finite transitive closure from nonzero $a$ is
\[
 \{y:\operatorname{sign}(y)=\operatorname{sign}(a),\ 0<|y|\le |a|\}.
\]
Necessity follows from sign preservation and monotone amplitude.  Conversely,
every such $y$ is reached at
\[
 T=\frac{y^{-2}-a^{-2}}{2\gamma}\ge0,
\]
and the finite time $T$ can be split into finitely many increments no larger
than $\Delta$.  The reachable set is therefore independent of the positive
magnitudes $\gamma$, $\Delta$, and $a_\star$.

This separation is sharpened by two controls.  At $\gamma=0$ the identity
flow still preserves both components and selects neither.  For $\gamma<0$ the
displayed real flow can lose global forward existence at finite time.  Hence
$\gamma>0$ prepares the particular global dissipative family but is not a
component selector.

\subsection{Reflection proves token nonselection}

The map
\[
 J(a,\sigma)=(-a,-\sigma)
\]
intertwines the flow, maps $U_\sigma$ to $U_{-\sigma}$, and satisfies
$b_{-\sigma}(-a)=b_\sigma(a)$.  On the two-element token set the reflection
has one orbit and no fixed token.  Equivariance therefore transports the pair
of candidates but selects neither member.  It proves no physical gauge
equivalence, no empirical equivalence, and no spontaneous symmetry breaking.

\subsection{Finite viability and the asymptotic boundary}

For a constant step $\Delta$ and $u_0=a_0^2>0$, exact iteration gives
\[
 u_n=\frac{u_0}{1+2\gamma n\Delta u_0}>0
\]
for each finite $n$.  For an arbitrary $n$-step path, the total time obeys
$0\le T\le n\Delta$, so the one-step argument at horizon $n\Delta$ gives the
greatest uniform factor
\[
 \Lambda_n=(1+2nH)^{-1/2},\qquad
 b_\sigma(a_n)\ge\Lambda_n b_\sigma(a_0)>0.
\]
Sharpness is approached at total time $n\Delta$ as $|a_0|\to\infty$.
Bernoulli's inequality gives $\Lambda_n\ge\lambda^n$, strictly for $n>1$.
This proves the stated finite-path viability with a sharper bound.  However,
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}u_n=0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}\PhiFlow(t,a)=0,
 \qquad
 \lim_{t\to\infty}b_\sigma(\PhiFlow(t,a))=0.
\]
Zero is not in $U_\sigma$, and $\Lambda_n$ has no positive lower bound
independent of $n$.  The fixed theorem therefore does not establish a closed
infinite-horizon viable limit or a positive asymptotic margin.  This precision
does not invalidate its finite-path statement.

\subsection{Component selection is not probability occurrence}

Fix any positive time $t_0$.  The bounded function
\[
 h(a)=\frac{a^2}{1+a^2}
\]
satisfies $h(\PhiFlow(t_0,a))<h(a)$ for $a\ne0$, with equality only at zero.
If a Borel probability $\mu$ is invariant, equality of the integrals of the
two sides forces $\mu(\{0\})=1$.  Thus the unique invariant probability is
$\delta_0$, whose support is outside both $U_+$ and $U_-$.  The token-relative
component selector supplies neither an initial amplitude, a probability law,
nor an event-occurrence rule.
