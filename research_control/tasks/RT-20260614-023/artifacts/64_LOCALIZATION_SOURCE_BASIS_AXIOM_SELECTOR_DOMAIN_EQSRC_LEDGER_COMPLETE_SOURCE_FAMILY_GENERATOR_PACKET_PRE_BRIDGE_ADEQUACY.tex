\begin{abstract}
This draft/control Ontology Formalizer artifact responds to
\texttt{handoff-0064}. It separates the retained formal relation status
of \(\EqLC\) on the declared \(\ObjLC\) certificate domain from the
scientific adequacy status needed before any downstream bridge. The
packet defines finite positive, negative, boundary, and
failed-certificate example obligations, a source-side readout-invariance
bridge ladder, and kill criteria for closure, repair, or later
promotion review. It does not discharge general \(\EqSrc\), does not
prove \(\RetainH\) or \(\GenH\), does not authorize downstream bridge
claims, does not reconstruct a candidate, does not edit canonical
ontology, does not promote benchmark status, does not request Gate Chair
review, and does not support completed-derivation language.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and Input State}

The input state is the completed \texttt{RT-20260614-022} Refuter stress
test. That test retained the formal relation surface only under literal
full-ledger preservation and reversible certificates. It placed the
scientific adequacy track in controlled pause because generator
coverage, certificate substance, quotient robustness, nontrivial
examples, failed examples, readout invariance, and bridge relevance
remain unproved.

This artifact does not add a source family and does not attempt a
downstream bridge. It defines what would have to be true before a later
bridge packet could be responsibly attempted.

\section{Two Status Tracks}

\begin{definition}[Formal relation status]
Formal relation status is the local algebraic claim
\[
    \EqLC \text{ is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive on } \ObjLC
\]
under the declared \(\CertLC\) rules. This status is conditional on
literal full-ledger preservation, inverse validity, and composition
closure. It is internal to the declared certificate domain.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scientific adequacy status]
Scientific adequacy status asks whether the same declared relation is
useful for later physics-bearing work. It requires nonvacuous source
examples, negative controls, failed certificates, quotient robustness,
source-pure readouts, and bridge relevance. Formal relation status alone
does not imply scientific adequacy.
\end{definition}

\begin{controlrule}[No status collapse]
The implication
\[
    \text{formal }\EqLC\text{ relation status}
    \Longrightarrow
    \text{scientific adequacy}
\]
is not established. Any use of formal status to infer general
\(\EqSrc\), \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), downstream bridge readiness, or
candidate reconstruction returns \(\Block\).
\end{controlrule}

\section{Adequacy Target}

\begin{definition}[Pre-bridge adequacy target]
The pre-bridge adequacy target \(\AdeqLC\) consists of five obligations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item a finite positive example whose certificate preserves all
    source ledger coordinates without target or benchmark selection;
    \item a finite negative example that is rejected because a ledger
    coordinate is weakened, erased, or selected downstream;
    \item a finite boundary example that is locally retained but cannot
    be generalized;
    \item a failed-certificate example where inverse validity or
    composition closure fails for source-side reasons; and
    \item a source-side readout ladder showing which readout maps are
    permitted, which invariance claims would be required, and which
    failures kill or repair the line.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{criterion}[Bridge attempt precondition]
A later bridge attempt is not admissible until \(\AdeqLC\) has been
audited and stress-tested. Passing \(\AdeqLC\) would still not by
itself discharge \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), or candidate reconstruction;
it would only permit a bounded bridge proposal to be considered.
\end{criterion}

\section{Finite Example Obligations}

\begin{examplecase}[Positive example obligation]
Supply \(X,Y\in\ObjLC\) and a certificate \(c:X\to Y\) such that every
ledger coordinate is explicitly transported:
\[
    \Ann,\ \Proxy,\ \Forbid,\ \Blocker,\ \Infl,\ q,\ C,\ i,\ o.
\]
The example must state the full object ledgers, the certificate steps,
the inverse certificate, and the composed identity checks. It must not
choose \(X\), \(Y\), a quotient representative, or a readout because of
target success, benchmark rank, observer protocol, proper time, or
candidate behavior.
\end{examplecase}

\begin{examplecase}[Negative example obligation]
Supply a near miss \(X,Y'\) where a positive relation would require
guard weakening, blocker erasure, influence deletion, boundary movement,
or downstream quotient selection. The required verdict is \(\Block\).
This example demonstrates that \(\ObjLC\) and \(\CertLC\) still reject
source-laundering moves.
\end{examplecase}

\begin{examplecase}[Boundary example obligation]
Supply a case such as the tri-family embedding where a local relation is
retained only inside a declared finite domain. The example must state
why local retention does not generalize to all source objects and why it
does not discharge \(\RetainH\) or \(\GenH\).
\end{examplecase}

\begin{examplecase}[Failed-certificate obligation]
Supply a finite certificate attempt that fails because the inverse loses
ledger provenance or because two individually plausible steps do not
compose over the same boundary and quotient data. The expected verdict
is \(\Block\) or \(\Repair\), not \(\Pass\).
\end{examplecase}

\section{Readout-Invariance Bridge Ladder}

\begin{definition}[Source-side readout map]
A source-side readout map is a function
\[
    \ReadSrc:\ObjLC\to R
\]
whose domain, codomain, and computation are defined only from source
ledger coordinates. It may use branch tokens, labels, annotations,
guards, blockers, influence records, quotient data, contraction data,
and boundary anchors. It may not use target metric, target proper time,
target locality, observer protocol, benchmark rank, candidate
performance, validation status, generated visibility, or authority
labels.
\end{definition}

\begin{obligation}[Ladder rung 0]
Define the intended source-side readout \(\ReadSrc\) and prove it is
well formed on \(\ObjLC\). Without this, bridge work remains undefined.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Ladder rung 1]
Prove certificate invariance:
\[
    X\EqLC Y \Longrightarrow \ReadSrc(X)=\ReadSrc(Y).
\]
This is a source-side theorem only. It does not yet identify the readout
with \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), clock behavior, causal structure, matter
coupling, or benchmark recovery.
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Ladder rung 2]
Audit the readout definition for hidden target primitives and authority
label laundering. If the readout depends on target metric, proper time,
locality, observer protocol, benchmark rank, or candidate behavior, the
line returns \(\Kill\) or \(\Repair\).
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Ladder rung 3]
Stress-test the readout under quotient variation, guard weakening,
blocker erasure, influence deletion, boundary-anchor perturbation,
non-chain refinement, and failed certificates. A readout that survives
only by deleting adverse ledger data returns \(\Block\).
\end{obligation}

\begin{obligation}[Ladder rung 4]
Only after the source-side readout survives audit and stress may a later
packet ask whether the readout is relevant to \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\),
or a bridge-recovery burden. That later packet would still be
draft/control and would not be a Gate Chair verdict.
\end{obligation}

\section{Kill and Repair Criteria}

\begin{criterion}[Kill criteria]
The line should be closed for this branch if any of the following are
required for a positive result:
\begin{itemize}
    \item target metric, proper time, target locality, observer
    protocol, benchmark rank, or candidate behavior as source data;
    \item validation status, registry presence, generated wiki
    visibility, or handoff status as mathematical evidence;
    \item deletion of guards, blockers, influence records, quotient
    provenance, or boundary anchors;
    \item nontrivial examples that cannot be supplied without
    downstream selection; or
    \item a readout map whose invariance fails under a valid
    source-ledger certificate.
\end{itemize}
\end{criterion}

\begin{criterion}[Repair criteria]
The line may be repaired only by adding source-side definitions or
certificates that preserve all claim boundaries. Repairs may include a
machine-checkable certificate algebra, a tighter quotient rule, an
explicit inverse checker, an influence-preservation lemma, or a
source-pure readout definition. Repair does not authorize promotion.
\end{criterion}

\begin{criterion}[Future promotion precondition]
Promotion review remains closed unless later audited and refuted
packets supply a source-pure readout, a nonvacuous invariance theorem,
negative examples that remain blocked, failed certificates that fail for
source-side reasons, and explicit bridge relevance. Even if those
conditions are met, Gate Chair review remains human-gated.
\end{criterion}
