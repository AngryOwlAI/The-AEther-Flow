\section{Arbitrarily small cohomology stress}

$\omega(e_{01})=1$ and zero on the other two displayed generators, with inverse

\]

\begin{theorem}[Transverse $H^1$ instability]
For every $\varepsilon$, $\rho_R^\varepsilon$ is a positive
one-dimensional representation with valid identity, inverse, and composition.
For every nonzero $\varepsilon$, however, its cycle log holonomy is
$3\mu+\varepsilon$, while the other two sector log holonomies remain $3\mu$.
Consequently the three sectors descend neither to one common positive
character nor to the fixed $\chi$.  The failure occurs for arbitrarily small
$|\varepsilon|$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Additivity of $\omega$ preserves the representation law and inverse negation.
The perturbation contributes $\varepsilon$ once around the displayed cycle.
Positive object cochains telescope around a cycle and therefore cannot alter
holonomy.  Unequal sector holonomies preclude any common intertwining
character.  Since nonzero $\varepsilon$ may be arbitrarily small, no open
neighborhood in the independent three-sector $H^1$ class space is contained
in the common-descent locus.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Codimension and anchor limit]
The locus for descent to some common character is the diagonal in the
three-sector log-holonomy space and has codimension two.  Equality to a fixed
character class imposes three equations in the four-class space and has
codimension three.  Erasing, changing, or restoring the anchors selects
intertwiner representatives only after holonomy equality holds; it cannot
repair the transverse $H^1$ defect.
\end{corollary}

For a quantitative coordinate diagnostic, equip the displayed log-holonomy
spaces with their standard Euclidean norms.  Orthogonal projection gives
\[
 \operatorname{dist}^2\bigl((h+\varepsilon,h,h),\operatorname{diag}\R^3\bigr)

With $\chi$ fixed, the sector-class distance is $|\varepsilon|$.  These are

This is an enlarged-family robustness obstruction, not an inconsistency inside

\section{Rank-preserving balance-law family}

Put $r_1=m_0$, $r_2=(1,1,-1,-1)$, and for $0\le s\le1$ define
\[

Every $B_s$ annihilates $d$ and has rank two.  Define
\[

\]

\begin{theorem}[Balance rank does not protect descent relevance]
For every $s\in[0,1]$, $B_sw_s=0$ and $w_s$ is not proportional to $d$.
Moreover,
\[
 m(w_s)=4(1-s).
\]
For $s<1$, $\operatorname{rank}\binom{B_s}{m}=3$; at $s=1$, the balance
matrix still has rank two but $m$ enters its row span, the induced covector on
$\ker B_s/\operatorname{span}\{d\}$ vanishes, and descent relevance collapses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Direct multiplication gives $B_sw_s=0$ and the displayed value of $m(w_s)$.
The rows $r_1,r_2,m$ are independent in the three-dimensional annihilator of
$d$.  Hence $m$ joins the row span of $B_s$ only at $s=1$, where the second row
equals $m$.  The two rows of $B_s$ remain independent throughout the interval.
\end{proof}

This finite family does not contradict RT011's local openness lemma near the
fixed coefficients.  It proves the narrower and complementary fact that rank
two, gauge preservation, and quotient dimension alone do not protect the
nonzero coupling between the balance quotient and the character covector.
