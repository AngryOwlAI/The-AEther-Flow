\section{Stress Target}

The stressed object is the finite certificate-fiber quotient
\[
  \MSCertQuot(E;\Bcur,C_+)=
  \left(\mathcal F_{\Gamma}(C_+;E,\Bcur),\MSCertEq,
  \pi^{\mathrm{cert}}_E,D^q_E\right).
\]
It identifies the pressure pair \(C_+\) and \(C_-\) because they share source
support, probe-token family, and no-target certificate bundle inside one
finite fiber while their source readout and response-marker labels differ.
The stress question is whether this quotient is determined, stable, and
nondegenerate enough to proceed as a source-side matter-semantics law
candidate.

\section{Stress Criteria}

The Refuter applies five bounded tests:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item collapse nonuniqueness;
  \item finite-variation fragility;
  \item certificate deletion;
  \item sector deletion;
  \item quotient degeneracy.
\end{enumerate}
These tests use only the candidate text and tracked source-side constraints.
They do not import target metrics, detector semantics, stress-energy tensors,
matter actions, benchmark behavior, or Einstein-equation premises.

\section{Stress Results}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse nonuniqueness]
The current source basis does not uniquely determine \(\MSCertEq\) as the
required quotient relation.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Reason.}
The same finite data allow at least three source-side relations: the identity
relation preserving all readout and response-marker syntax, the proposed
certificate-fiber relation ignoring those labels inside the fiber, and a
coarser all-fiber collapse. The current ontology supplies no source-side law
that selects the middle relation as the unique matter-semantics irrelevance
relation. Thus the quotient is a choice of proposal-only compression, not a
derived law.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-variation fragility]
The quotient is not stable under finite variations of the declared support,
probe-token, or certificate-fiber data unless an additional repair law is
added.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Reason.}
Changing one support token, one probe token, or one certificate-bundle member
can move a record outside \(\mathcal F_{\Gamma}(C_+;E,\Bcur)\) and therefore
to \(\bot\), or can split a previously collapsed class. The candidate has a
fail-closed boundary, but fail-closed behavior is not a finite-variation
robustness theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Certificate-deletion failure]
Deleting a no-target certificate breaks the domain guard and does not preserve
a nonbottom quotient class.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Reason.}
Certificate preservation is a defining condition of the fiber relation. If
one of the no-target certificates is removed, the candidate does not derive an
invariant quotient on the remaining source data. It fails closed. This is
safe, but it is not a stable matter-semantics law.

\begin{proposition}[Sector-deletion instability]
Deleting a finite sector of records from the candidate fiber changes the
available quotient classes and is not shown to commute with
\(\pi^{\mathrm{cert}}_E\).
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Reason.}
The quotient is defined extensionally by membership in a finite fiber. Removing
records or subfamilies changes the finite fiber itself. No functorial
sector-deletion rule, invariance lemma, or recovery map is supplied by the
current candidate.

\begin{proposition}[Quotient degeneracy]
The proposed discriminator \(D^q_E\) is identical to the quotient projection
and therefore erases the readout and response-marker distinctions that later
matter-semantics reasoning would need to justify nontrivial coupling.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Reason.}
Inside the fiber, \(D^q_E(C)=\pi^{\mathrm{cert}}_E(C)\). For the pressure
pair, this deliberately returns the same quotient value for \(C_+\) and
\(C_-\). That solves the local collapse target by definition, but it leaves no
source-intrinsic criterion for which readout or response-marker distinctions
matter elsewhere. The degeneracy is not repaired by target semantics, because
target semantics are forbidden inputs.

\section{Obstruction}

\begin{definition}[Scoped quotient obstruction]
\[
  \Ob(E;\Bcur,C_+)
\]
is the statement that, under the current source basis, \(\MSCertQuot\) is not
uniquely selected, finite-variation robust, certificate-deletion stable,
sector-deletion invariant, and nondegenerate enough to serve as an adopted
matter-semantics irrelevance law.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Scoped obstruction verdict]
\(\Ob(E;\Bcur,C_+)\) holds for \(\MSCertQuot\) as written.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
The five stress propositions above each identify an independent failure mode.
Together they refute the candidate as a robust or adoptable source-side law
under current inputs. They do not refute the possibility of a future stronger
source law, a redesigned quotient with added invariance data, or a protected
human-gated ontology change.
