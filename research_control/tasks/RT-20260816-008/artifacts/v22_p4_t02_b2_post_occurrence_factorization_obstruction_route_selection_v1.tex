functor.  A natural transformation $s:1\Rightarrow F$ assigns
$s_d\in F(d)$ such that $F(g)s_d=s_e$ for every $g:d\to e$.

\paragraph{Theorem.}
On each connected component of $\mathcal G$, natural sections are in bijection
with the points of one representative fibre fixed by its full isotropy group.

\paragraph{Proof.}
If $s$ is natural and $h\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal G}(d_0)$, then
$F(h)s_{d_0}=s_{d_0}$, so the representative value is fixed.  Conversely,
choose an isotropy-fixed $a_0\in F(d_0)$ and define
$s_d=F(g)a_0$ for any $g:d_0\to d$.  If $g'$ is another such arrow, then
$g^{-1}g'$ is an automorphism of $d_0$; fixedness makes the definition
independent of the arrow.  Functoriality supplies naturality.  Repeat on each
component. \hfill$\square$
