\section{Conditional theorem retained}

\begin{theorem}[Compact positive lower-semicontinuous minimum]
Let \(\mathcal R\) be nonempty and compact, and let
\(\kappa:\mathcal R\to\R_{>0}\) be finite and lower semicontinuous.  Then
\[
  \kappa_*:=\min_{r\in\mathcal R}\kappa(r)
\]
exists, is attained, and is strictly positive.  If an \(\EqSrc\) arrow acts
by a homeomorphism and transports \(\kappa\) naturally, it preserves
\(\kappa_*\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A finite real-valued lower-semicontinuous function attains its minimum on a
nonempty compact space.  The attained value is one of the strictly positive
point values and is therefore positive.  Natural homeomorphic transport
preserves the value set, hence also its minimum.
\end{proof}

The theorem is exact, but its hypotheses do not derive themselves.  It earns
no current-ontology, physical, empirical, adoption, or Distance-to-GR credit.

\section{Same-reduct nonselection theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Two admissible expansions do not select a generator]
Fix the canonical reduct
\(B_{\mathrm{src}}=(\text{smooth four-manifold},\Phi_{\mathrm{src}}
\text{ unresolved})\).  It has both of the following finite formal
expansions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item one presentation object with its identity arrow, one root \(r_0\),
  and \(\kappa(r_0)=1\);
  \item one presentation object with a \(C_2\) automorphism, roots
  \(r_0,r_1\), the automorphism swapping them, and the orbit-constant values
  \(\kappa(r_0)=\kappa(r_1)=1\).
\end{enumerate}
Both root spaces are nonempty finite discrete and therefore compact.  Both
moduli are finite, positive, continuous, and natural; the nontrivial arrow
squares to the identity.  The two expansions are nonisomorphic because their
root fibres have different cardinalities.  Hence the canonical reduct alone
selects neither \(\mathsf{CompleteRoot}\) nor \(\GSrc\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Forgetting the added presentation, root, action, and modulus symbols sends
both structures to the same canonical reduct.  All displayed proposal axioms
hold by direct finite calculation.  Any construction uniquely definable from
the reduct would have invariant isomorphism type across its admissible
expansions.  Cardinalities one and two contradict that requirement.
\end{proof}

A third expansion can keep the two-root swap and use the orbit-constant value
\(\kappa\equiv2\).  This preserves every structural condition while changing
the normalization, so the reduct also fails to select the modulus profile.

\(\kappa(n)=1/(n+1)\) has infimum zero.  Pointwise positivity without
compactness does not imply a uniform positive bound.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Compact non-lower-semicontinuous control]
Let
\[
  \mathcal R=\{0\}\cup\{1/n:n\geq1\},\qquad
  \kappa(0)=1,\quad \kappa(1/n)=1/n.
\]
Then \(\mathcal R\) is compact and \(\kappa\) is pointwise positive, but
\(\inf\kappa=0\).  It is not lower semicontinuous at zero.
\end{proposition}

The outcome-filter control begins with roots
\(\{r_{\mathrm{pass}},r_{\mathrm{fail}}\}\) and then retains only
\(r_{\mathrm{pass}}\).  That changes the total family.  Thus defining roots
as successful cases, defining \(\kappa\) as a frozen diagnostic margin, or
restricting operations to those that preserve the desired outcome is not
source generation; it is circular filtering and frozen-route replay.
