\section{Extension-Collapse Stress}

\begin{definition}[Extension-collapse map]
Let \(F_X(\Splus)=\SX\) be the forgetful reduct that deletes exactly
\(\XiR\). The extension-collapse stress evaluates the candidate after
applying \(F_X\).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse restores the old obstruction]
The selector \(\SelResp^{+}\) does not induce a selector
\[
  \SX \mapsto (o_X^R,\nu_X^R,\kappa_X^R)
\]
after extension collapse.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction, \(\SelResp^{+}\) reads the three distinguished
components of \(\XiR\). Applying \(F_X\) removes \(O_X^R,N_X^R,K_X^R\)
and \(o_X^R,\nu_X^R,\kappa_X^R\). The old retained tuple \(\SX\) then
contains only \(U_X,[n]_{U_X},\Phi_{U_X},T^n,T^\Phi\). The prior scoped
selector no-go and finite toy stress results already preserve that
those old tuple components do not determine response orientation,
positive normalization, or response-token semantics under the
no-target-import contract. Therefore the expanded selector collapses to
the old obstruction rather than deriving a selector from \(\SX\).
\end{proof}

\section{Tag-Removal Stress}

\begin{definition}[Selector-data erasure]
Let \(E_R\) be the operation that removes one or more of
\(o_X^R,\nu_X^R,\kappa_X^R\) while leaving the old tuple \(\SX\)
unchanged.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Tag-removal destroys the candidate selector]
If \(E_R\) removes \(o_X^R\), \(\nu_X^R\), or \(\kappa_X^R\), then
\(\SelResp^{+}(E_R(\Splus))\) is not a complete response-selector
triple.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The definition of \(\SelResp^{+}\) returns exactly
\((o_X^R,\nu_X^R,\kappa_X^R)\). If one component is absent, the returned
object is either undefined or incomplete as a response-selector triple.
No rule in the old tuple reconstructs the missing component. Thus the
candidate depends exactly on explicit selector-extension data.
\end{proof}

This finding is not a defect in the candidate as a source-extension
candidate. It is the reason the candidate cannot be reclassified as a
derivation from the current retained tuple.
