\[

\[
L_p=\langle p\rangle,\qquad
L_q=\langle q\rangle,\qquad
L_d=\langle d\rangle.
\]
For a line \(L\), define the congruence
\[
x\,R_L\,y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x-y\in L.
\]

ordered pairs.

Let \(c(p)=q\), \(c(q)=p\), and \(c(d)=d\). An equivariant line family on the
two-element orientation torsor is determined by its line over one

\[
(L_q,L_p),\qquad(L_p,L_q),\qquad(L_d,L_d).
\]
The first is the candidate family. Each family satisfies the same
orientation-change cocycle, descends to a line on the associated state, and

\begin{proof}

\section{Full linear and affine census}

\begin{theorem}[Linear orbit]
\(\operatorname{GL}(2,2)\) has order six and acts transitively on
\(\{L_p,L_q,L_d\}\). No nonzero proper line is invariant under the full
linear group.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An invertible map is determined by the image of an ordered basis: there are
three choices for the first nonzero image and two remaining choices for the
second. Given any two nonzero vectors, extend each to a basis and map one
basis to the other. Hence the three lines form one orbit.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Affine stabilizers and their intersection]
\(\operatorname{AGL}(2,2)\) has order \(24\). For each line \(L\), the
stabilizer of \(R_L\) has order eight. The intersection of the three
stabilizers is exactly the four-element translation group.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
An affine map has the form \(x\mapsto Mx+t\), with six choices of \(M\) and
four of \(t\). It preserves \(R_L\) precisely when \(M(L)=L\), because the
translation cancels from differences. The linear stabilizer of one line has
order two, giving \(2\cdot4=8\) affine stabilizers. A linear map stabilizing
all three lines fixes all three nonzero vectors and is the identity. The
translation remains arbitrary.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Translation nondiscrimination]

candidate's declared translations do not distinguish its line.
\end{corollary}
