\begin{abstract}
The fixed current-smooth-source theorem assigns an objectwise natural proper
arrow subgroup $\Kstar(M)$ to every nonempty smooth four-manifold.  This note
proves that the unchanged assignment is not restriction-exhaustive on a
compact-manifold/coordinate-ball pair, that its local compact-support
stabilizer realizes $GL^{+}(4)$ first jets and therefore selects no nonzero
vector, covector, proper cone, Lorentzian conformal cone, orientation sheet,
or nontrivial scalar response, and that $\Diff/\Diffc$ erases compact local
bumps while retaining end data.  Consequently $\Kstar$ alone has no
standalone local bridge force in the predeclared grammar.  This is a scoped
source-side irrelevance theorem, not a global no-go against enriched source
structure.  It assigns no physical, empirical, ontological, or promotion
status.
\end{abstract}

\section{Compact/open restriction nonexhaustion}

\begin{theorem}[Compact/open witness]
Let $M$ be a compact smooth connected four-manifold and let $U\Subset M$ be
a coordinate ball.  Then the restrictions of arrows in $\Kstar(M)$ do not
exhaust $\Kstar(U)$.  In particular the printed objectwise assignment is not
a restriction-exhaustive presheaf on this pair.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every diffeomorphism of compact $M$ has compact support, because its support
is closed in a compact space.  Hence $\Diffc(M)=\Diff(M)$ and
$\Kstar(M)=\{\operatorname{id}_{M}\}$.

The open ball $U$ is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{R}^{4}$.  Transporting a
translation of $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ to $U$ gives a diffeomorphism whose support is
not compact in $U$, while a nonzero compactly supported smooth vector field
on $U$ has a nonidentity small-time flow.  Therefore
$\Diffc(U)\subsetneq\Diff(U)$ and $\Kstar(U)=\Diffc(U)$ is nontrivial.

Choose nonidentity $h\in\Diffc(U)$.  Since its support is compactly contained
in $U$, extension by the identity gives a smooth nonidentity
$\widetilde h\in\Diff(M)$.  But $\widetilde h\notin\Kstar(M)$, and the only
restriction coming from $\Kstar(M)$ is $\operatorname{id}_{U}$.  Thus the
restriction image is a proper subgroup of $\Kstar(U)$.
\end{proof}

The result concerns Equation~\eqref{eq:kstar} as printed.  An enriched source
theory could add restriction data or choose another local object; this theorem
does not rule out such an extension.

\section{Local first-jet isotropy}

\begin{lemma}[$GL^{+}(4)$ jet realization]
Let $U$ be a coordinate ball, $x\in U$, and
$\operatorname{Stab}_{c}(x)\subset\Diffc(U)$ the stabilizer of $x$.  Its
derivative image at $x$ contains all of $GL^{+}(T_{x}U)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in coordinates with $x=0$.  For $A\in GL^{+}(4)$, choose a smooth path
$A_t$ in $GL^{+}(4)$ from $I$ to $A$.  Let
$B_t=\dot A_tA_t^{-1}$ and choose a compactly supported bump $\chi$ that is
one near zero.  The time-dependent field
\[
X_t(y)=\chi(y)B_ty
\]
is compactly supported, fixes zero, and has a globally defined time-one flow.
Near zero its derivative solves
$\dot D\varphi_t(0)=B_tD\varphi_t(0)$ with initial value $I$, hence
$D\varphi_t(0)=A_t$.  The time-one map lies in
$\operatorname{Stab}_{c}(0)$ and realizes $A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No canonical vector, covector, or proper cone]
No nonzero tangent vector or cotangent covector at $x$ is fixed by the local
$\Kstar(U)$ stabilizer.  No proper nonempty subset of
$T_xU\setminus\{0\}$, and hence no Lorentzian conformal null or causal cone,
is invariant under the full realized first-jet group.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The positive scalar $2I\in GL^{+}(4)$ sends $v$ to $2v$ and pulls a covector
$\alpha$ back to $2\alpha$.  Fixedness forces $v=0$ and $\alpha=0$.
Moreover $GL^{+}(4)$ acts transitively on the nonzero vectors: extend any
nonzero source and target vectors to bases and adjust one complementary basis
vector so that the resulting linear map has positive determinant.  An
invariant subset of the punctured space is therefore empty or the entire
punctured space, not a proper cone.  A Lorentzian conformal cone is proper, so
it cannot be invariant under this group.
\end{proof}

Compactly supported diffeomorphisms preserve each of the two mathematical
orientation sheets, so their common stabilizer does not choose between the
sheets.  Mathematical orientation is not physical time orientation.  Also,
$\Diffc(U)$ acts transitively on connected $U$ by compactly supported flows
along embedded paths.  An invariant scalar function is therefore constant,
and Equation~\eqref{eq:kstar} selects neither its value nor an empirical
interpretation.  Thus the remaining two grammar candidates also have no
selector force from $\Kstar$ alone.

\section{The large quotient is not a local repair}

\begin{proposition}[Local information/end-data separation]
On a noncompact smooth manifold, $\Diffc(M)$ is normal in $\Diff(M)$.  On
$M=\mathbb{R}^{4}$, the quotient $\Diff(M)/\Diffc(M)$ kills compactly
supported local first-jet changes while distinguishing representatives that
have the same germ near the origin and different behavior at infinity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $f\in\Diff(M)$ and $h\in\Diffc(M)$,
\[
\operatorname{supp}(fhf^{-1})=f(\operatorname{supp}h),
\]
which is compact, proving normality.  Every compactly supported local bump is
therefore in the neutral quotient class, so the quotient erases its local
germ and first-jet information.

For the converse separation, choose a smooth strictly increasing radial
function $R:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ equal to $r$ near zero and $2r$ for all
sufficiently large $r$.  The radial diffeomorphism
$g(x)=R(|x|)x/|x|$, with $g(0)=0$, equals the identity near zero but is not
compactly supported.  Hence $g$ and the identity have the same declared local
germ and distinct quotient classes.  The quotient retains end data, not a
local response selector.
\end{proof}

\section{Scoped standalone-bridge irrelevance}

\begin{theorem}[$\Kstar$ standalone local-bridge irrelevance]
Within the six-candidate grammar and under the stated compact/open and chart
hypotheses, the unchanged assignment $\Kstar$ supplies no nontrivial
restriction-compatible local bridge by itself.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The compact/open theorem shows that objectwise conjugation naturality does not
supply the restriction-exhaustive local family required for gluing.  On an
open chart, the realized $GL^{+}(4)$ isotropy fixes no nonzero vector or
covector and no proper nonempty cone, including a Lorentzian conformal cone.
The common orientation-preserving stabilizer preserves both orientation
sheets and chooses neither.  Invariant scalar readouts are arbitrary
constants, and $\Kstar$ chooses no value.  Finally the large quotient erases
compact local changes and distinguishes end changes, so it does not restore
the missing local selector.  Every predeclared candidate therefore fails at
least one necessary condition before physical interpretation is considered.
\end{proof}

This theorem is intentionally narrow.  It does not prove that no enriched
source structure can yield a local bridge, that $\Kstar$ has no diagnostic
use, or that the wider project is impossible.  It proves only that the
unchanged assignment, without an independently sourced codomain, local map,
restriction law, and gluing law, has no standalone bridge force in the fixed
grammar.
