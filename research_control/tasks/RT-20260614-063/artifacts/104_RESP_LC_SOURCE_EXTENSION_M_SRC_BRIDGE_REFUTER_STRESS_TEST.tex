\section{Chart-Germ Collapse}

\begin{definition}[Local-germ collapse]

label data \(\XiR\) or the response-labelled prechart structure

\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Collapse removes the local bridge]
The local source-chart germ does not survive \(G_X\) as a response-labelled
candidate toward \(\Msrc\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The defining formula for \(\chi_X^+\) uses \(o_X^R\), \(\nu_X^R\), and
\(\kappa_X^R\) from \(\XiR\). If those labels or their prechart role are
forgotten, the object no longer contains a response-labelled local prechart.

preserve that the old tuple alone does not determine response orientation,
normalization, token semantics, or a source-manifold atlas. Therefore the
local bridge is not derivable from the old exact tuple by collapse.
\end{proof}

\section{Atlas-Glue Underdetermination}

\begin{definition}[Atlas-glue obligation]
A full source-manifold packet requires a source-only rule \(\AtlasGlue\) that
supplies patch coverage, overlap recognition, transition maps, compatibility
on overlaps, and finite-variation stability for local germs
\(\Mloc_X,\Mloc_Y,\ldots\).
\end{definition}

\begin{obstruction}[Local germs do not determine atlas glue]
The current \(\Splus\) local-germ data do not determine \(\AtlasGlue\).
\end{obstruction}

\begin{proof}
The local germ contains one carrier \(U_X\), one source prechart
\(\chi_X^+\), the exact local certificate family, exact transports, and the
adopted response-extension datum. A gluing rule for multiple independently
constructed patches additionally needs a source-only criterion for when
patches overlap, a transition operation \(\tauXY\), compatibility on triple
overlaps, and stability under finite source variations. None of those objects
is defined by the single local germ, by the response labels alone, or by
validation status. Supplying them from a target manifold, target smooth atlas,
target transition map, metric scale, empirical calibration, or benchmark
solution would violate the no-target-import boundary. Hence the atlas-glue
burden remains underdetermined.
\end{proof}

\section{Transition-Law Nonuniqueness}

\begin{proposition}[Schematic nonuniqueness]
Without \(\AtlasGlue\), two local response-labelled germs can admit
incompatible transition choices while each remains locally well formed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Consider two local germs \(\Mloc_X\) and \(\Mloc_Y\) whose response labels are
read as source-extension tokens. A putative transition rule may preserve
shared response labels identically, or it may rescale or reindex those labels
by a source-token automorphism that preserves each local prechart syntax but
changes compatibility on overlaps. The current packets provide no source-only
criterion selecting one transition law, rejecting the other, or proving that
the difference is gauge-equivalent. Therefore local source-purity does not
determine a unique atlas transition structure.
\end{proof}

This is a schematic countermodel, not a target-geometry construction. It
shows that the local germ and audit pass do not by themselves supply the
transition-law burden for \(\Msrc\).

\section{Finite-Variation Fragility}

The exact local certificate \(\Cexact\) is an exact-branch object. It does not
prove that overlaps, transition laws, and compatibility data persist under
finite source variations. A full \(\Msrc\) result would need either a
finite-variation-stable \(\AtlasGlue\) rule or a theorem showing that the
needed atlas data are invariant under the allowed source variations. Neither
payload is supplied by the audited local germ.
