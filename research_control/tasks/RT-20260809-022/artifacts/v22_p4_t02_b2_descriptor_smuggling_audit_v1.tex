\section{Source-fiber factorization criterion}

\begin{definition}[Audit input split]
Let
\[
  X=S_{\src}\times T_{\mathrm{target}}\times A_{\mathrm{authority}},
  \qquad \pi_S:X\longrightarrow S_{\src},
\]
where $S_{\src}$ contains the permitted source objects,
$T_{\mathrm{target}}$ contains forbidden target-atlas, metric, desired-cone,
signature, scale, or benchmark data, and $A_{\mathrm{authority}}$ contains
roles, registries, generated derivatives, validation outcomes, Gate state, and
workflow state.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Source-determined audit term]
An audit term $F:X\to Y$ is source-determined when it is constant on every
fiber of $\pi_S$:
\[
  \pi_S(x)=\pi_S(x')\quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(x)=F(x').
\]
Equivalently, there is a map $F_{\src}$ on the image of $\pi_S$ such that
$F=F_{\src}\circ\pi_S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Factorization test]
The fiber-constancy condition and the displayed factorization condition are
equivalent.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Factorization immediately gives fiber constancy.  Conversely, define
$F_{\src}(s)$ as $F(x)$ for any $x$ with $\pi_S(x)=s$.  Fiber constancy makes
this definition independent of the representative, and substitution gives
$F=F_{\src}\circ\pi_S$.
\end{proof}

This elementary criterion is useful because it separates three questions that
must not be collapsed:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Is the term invariant under target and authority changes at fixed
        source input?
  \item Is the source term actually constructed and internally justified?
  \item Is that source construction adopted or physically interpreted?
\end{enumerate}
This audit can answer the first question for the schema.  It cannot answer the
second for an unpopulated instance and has no authority to answer the third.

\section{A precise uniqueness obstruction}

The formalization also states, in effect, that any other family with the same
reduced local hypersurfaces differs locally by nowhere-zero units.  Real
zero-set equality alone is insufficient.

\begin{counterexample}[Same real projective hypersurface, no unit association]
On a real four-dimensional source covector space with coordinates
$(k_0,k_1,k_2,k_3)$, let
\[
  P(k)=k_0,
  \qquad
  Q(k)=k_0\bigl(k_0^2+k_1^2\bigr).
\]
Both polynomials are homogeneous and square-free over $\mathbb{R}$.  Their
real projective zero sets are equal.  Indeed,
\[
 Q(k)=0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 k_0=0\ \text{or}\ k_0^2+k_1^2=0,
\]
and the second real locus has $k_0=k_1=0$, which is already contained in
$P(k)=0$.  Nevertheless,
\[
  \frac{Q}{P}=k_0^2+k_1^2
\]
vanishes on the nonempty projective locus $k_0=k_1=0$ and depends on the
covector.  It is not a nowhere-zero overlap source unit.
\end{counterexample}

Therefore the uniqueness-from-real-zero-set addendum receives
\fail{} scope.  A later construction may instead provide explicit unit
association, as the core theorem already requires.  Alternatively it may
work with equal appropriate locally principal characteristic ideals and a
minimal-generator convention in a ring where equal principal ideals force
unit association.  Merely replacing ``zero set'' by ``real radical zero set''
does not supply the needed unit.

This obstruction does not refute the conditional gluing core.  It prevents a
constructor from claiming that the unit and cocycle evidence follows for free
from equality of real characteristic hypersurfaces.
