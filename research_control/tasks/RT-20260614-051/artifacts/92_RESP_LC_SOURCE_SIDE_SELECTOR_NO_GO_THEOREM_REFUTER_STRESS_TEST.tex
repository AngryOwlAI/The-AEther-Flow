\begin{abstract}
This draft/control Refuter artifact stress-tests the scoped
\(\RespLC\) source-side selector no-go theorem from
\texttt{RT-20260614-050}. The theorem says that the retained tuple
\[
    S_X=(U_X,[n]_{U_X},\Phi_{U_X},T^n,T^\Phi)
\]
does not determine response sign orientation \(o^R_X\), positive
response normalization \(\nu^R_X\), or response-token semantics
\(\kappa^R_X\) under source-invariance and the audited
no-target-import selector contract. The stress test finds that the
theorem survives as a scoped obstruction. Arbitrary conventions do not
defeat it because convention is not source determination. A future
source extension may evade the theorem by changing the input domain,
but that is not a counterexample to a theorem about \(S_X\) alone and
is not authorized here as canonical ontology. The registered
loop-risk category is \texttt{scoped\_obstruction}; the next route is
\texttt{theoretical\_decision\_role\_selection}. No canonical
\(\RespLC\), \(\Msrc\), \(\geff\), matter coupling, Einstein
equations, \(\EqSrc\), \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), finite-variation
robustness, benchmark promotion, Gate Chair review, candidate
reconstruction, completed derivation, future source-extension
impossibility, or global theory rejection is supplied.
\end{abstract}

\section{Stress Method}

\begin{stressscreen}[Hypothesis-strength pressure]
Test whether the theorem wins only by building the desired answer into
its hypotheses. The target hypotheses are source-invariance and
no-target-import selector determination from \(S_X\).
\end{stressscreen}

\begin{stressscreen}[Arbitrary-convention loophole pressure]
Test whether a free choice of sign, unit, or token name can count as a
selector and therefore refute the non-determinacy result.
\end{stressscreen}

\begin{stressscreen}[Source-extension counterexample pressure]
Test whether a legitimate future source extension can supply selector
data and whether that would be a counterexample to the scoped theorem.
\end{stressscreen}

\begin{stressscreen}[Promotion and rejection overread pressure]

Gate Chair verdict, completed derivation, future source-extension
impossibility, or global theory rejection.
\end{stressscreen}

\section{Stress Findings}

\begin{stressfinding}[Hypotheses are strong but not circular]
The source-invariance and no-target-import hypotheses are strong. They
exclude target geometry, observer protocol, calibration, matter
coupling, benchmark evidence, and authority labels. That strength is
not a defect for the inspected question, because the selector contract
itself asks whether \(S_X\) determines the response selector without
those imports. The theorem does not prove more than that.
\end{stressfinding}

\begin{stressfinding}[Arbitrary convention is not a source selector]
A rule may name an arbitrary positive unit, sign orientation, or token
dictionary. Such a rule can coordinate notation, but it is not
determined by \(S_X\). If two conventions leave \(U_X\), \([n]_{U_X}\),
\(\Phi_{U_X}\), \(T^n\), and \(T^\Phi\) fixed while changing response
sign, scale, or token semantics, then \(S_X\) does not distinguish
them. Therefore arbitrary convention is not a counterexample to the
theorem.
\end{stressfinding}

\begin{stressfinding}[Source extension changes the theorem domain]
Consider a schematic extension
\[
    S_X^+=(S_X,O_X,N_X,K_X),
\]
where \(O_X\) is a source orientation datum, \(N_X\) is a source
positive-normalization datum, and \(K_X\) is a source response-token
semantics datum. If such objects were introduced through an authorized
source-language route, then \(S_X^+\) could determine a selector in a
way that \(S_X\) cannot. This would not refute the theorem about
\(S_X\) alone. It would create a new packet with new authority
questions.
\end{stressfinding}

\begin{stressfinding}[No positive witness is supplied]
The schematic \(S_X^+\) observation is only a domain-distinction
screen. It is not a concrete \(\RespLC\) witness, not a source-to-metric
law, and not a human-gated ontology edit. This Refuter job cannot
adopt \(O_X,N_X,K_X\) as canonical source primitives.
\end{stressfinding}

\begin{stressfinding}[Overread is blocked]
The theorem supplies a local obstruction to selector determination
from the current tuple. It does not supply \(\Msrc\), \(\geff\),
matter coupling, Einstein equations, finite-variation robustness,
benchmark success, or Gate Chair review. It also does not prove that
every future source language must fail.
\end{stressfinding}

\section{Scoped Obstruction}

\begin{obstruction}[The theorem survives as scoped obstruction]
The theorem survives the stress test in this exact scope:
\[
    S_X \nRightarrow (o^R_X,\nu^R_X,\kappa^R_X)
\]
under source-invariance and no-target-import selector determination.
The obstruction is the same source-invisible freedom in sign, positive
scale, and token semantics identified by the theorem.
\end{obstruction}

\begin{obstruction}[The escape route is external to the theorem]
The only live escape identified here is to add source-side selector
data or source-side primitives not present in \(S_X\). That route is
outside the theorem's input domain. If the project wants to pursue it,
the next step must select a bounded theoretical packet and preserve
the human gate for canonical ontology adoption.
\end{obstruction}
