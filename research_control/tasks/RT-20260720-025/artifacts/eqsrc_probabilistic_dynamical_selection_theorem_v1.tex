\section{Question and source-side setup}

The v21 P3-T04 question is whether probability or dynamics can replace a
missing deterministic selector without silently importing the selection.
Let a finite group \(G\) act on a nonempty finite selector set \(S\).  All
sets, actions, laws, decoders, initial data, boundaries, and readouts below
are declared before any desired downstream branch is inspected.  The action
is structural; no physical-gauge interpretation is assumed.

\begin{definition}[Probability targets]
Write
\[
 \Delta(S)=\left\{\mu:S\to\mathbb R_{\geq0}:\sum_{s\in S}\mu(s)=1\right\},
 \qquad (g_*\mu)(s)=\mu(g^{-1}s).
\]
A law is \(G\)-invariant when \(g_*\mu=\mu\) for all \(g\).  A point
law is a Dirac mass \(\delta_s\).  A probability law, a stationary law, a
realized sample, and the support point of a Dirac law are typed separately.
\end{definition}

\section{Probability does not provide a free point}

Let \(O_1,\ldots,O_k\) be the \(G\)-orbits in \(S\), and let \(u_O\)
denote the uniform probability law on orbit \(O\).

\begin{theorem}[Orbit-simplex classification]\label{thm:orbit-simplex}
The invariant probability laws are exactly
\[
 \Delta(S)^G=\operatorname{conv}\{u_{O_1},\ldots,u_{O_k}\}.
\]
Every invariant law has the unique form
\(\mu=\sum_i\alpha_i u_{O_i}\), where
\(\alpha_i=\mu(O_i)\geq0\) and \(\sum_i\alpha_i=1\).  Hence this
simplex has affine dimension \(k-1\), and the invariant law is unique if and
only if the action is transitive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Invariance makes \(\mu\) constant along every orbit.  If orbit \(O_i\)
has total mass \(\alpha_i\), each point of that orbit has mass
\(\alpha_i/|O_i|\).  Conversely, every orbit-uniform law and every convex
combination of them is invariant.  Disjoint orbit supports make the
coefficients unique.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Invariant Dirac criterion]\label{cor:dirac}
The Dirac law \(\delta_s\) is invariant if and only if
\(s\in S^G\), the common fixed locus.  In particular, a transitive action
on more than one point has a unique invariant law but no invariant point law.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because \(g_*\delta_s=\delta_{gs}\), invariance is equivalent to
\(gs=s\) for every \(g\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[No-free-measure decoder]\label{thm:decoder}
Let \(D\subseteq\Delta(S)\) be \(G\)-stable and let
\(d:D\to S\) be a deterministic equivariant decoder.  If
\(\mu\in D\) is invariant, then \(d(\mu)\in S^G\).  Thus when
\(S^G=\varnothing\), no such decoder converts an invariant probability law
into a lawful distinguished point.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every \(g\in G\), equivariance and invariance give
\(g d(\mu)=d(g_*\mu)=d(\mu)\).
\end{proof}

The theorem does not forbid a prospectively supplied decoder when a fixed
point exists.  It says that the decoder can return only already-fixed
structure, and any choice among several fixed points remains extra data.

\section{Dynamics preserves the symmetry burden}

Let \(G\) act on a nonempty finite state space \(\Omega\).  A Markov kernel
\(P\) is equivariant when
\(P(gx,gy)=P(x,y)\).  This is equivalent to
\(g_*(\nu P)=(g_*\nu)P\) for every state law \(\nu\).

\begin{theorem}[Equivariant Markov preservation]\label{thm:markov}
If \(P\) is equivariant and \(\nu_0\) is invariant, then
\(\nu_t=\nu_0P^t\) is invariant for every finite \(t\).  If
\(r:\Omega\to S\) is equivariant, then \(r_*\nu_t\) is invariant.
Any existing limit of these laws remains invariant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Commutation of pushforward with one Markov step and induction prove the
finite-time claim.  Equivariance of \(r\) commutes with pushforward.  The
finite invariant simplex is closed, so limits remain inside it.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unique stationary-law guard]\label{cor:stationary}
If an equivariant kernel has a unique stationary law \(\pi\), then \(\pi\)
is invariant.  It gives a deterministic selected point only if its selector
readout is a Dirac mass on a \(G\)-fixed point.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every \(g\), the law \(g_*\pi\) is stationary.  Uniqueness gives
\(g_*\pi=\pi\), and Corollary~\ref{cor:dirac} handles the Dirac case.
\end{proof}

Unique stationarity alone does not imply convergence.  Irreducibility and
aperiodicity, or another explicit convergence condition, must be stated.

\begin{theorem}[Unique global point attractor]\label{thm:attractor}
Let \(T:\Omega\to\Omega\) be equivariant.  If a point \(a\) is the
unique global fixed attractor---\(T(a)=a\) and every state reaches \(a\)
after finitely many iterations---then \(a\in\Omega^G\).  More generally,
\(G\) transports attractors and their basins into symmetry-related
attractors and basins.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equivariance makes \(ga\) fixed and maps every trajectory reaching \(a\)
to a trajectory reaching \(ga\).  Thus \(ga\) is also a global attractor;
uniqueness forces \(ga=a\).  The same calculation transports basins.
\end{proof}

A single run of an invariant stochastic process may end at a non-fixed
branch.  This does not contradict Theorem~\ref{thm:markov}: the ensemble law
can remain invariant while the realized initial state or noise history is
asymmetric.  That history is the datum which distinguishes the run.  Calling
the breaking ``spontaneous'' does not erase the initial, boundary,
environmental, limiting, or noise provenance that enters a basin.

\section{Provenance and interpretation}

A probabilistic or dynamical selector package must separately source:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
 \item the probability space and law \(M\);
 \item the state space, transition law, and evolution semantics \(D\);
 \item the initial state or initial law \(I\);
 \item boundary or asymptotic data \(B\);
 \item any realized random history, stopping rule, and selector readout.
\end{enumerate}
A non-invariant \(I\) or \(B\), a realized history, or a non-equivariant
\(D\) may conditionally select a branch, but its provenance must be retained.
The adopted exact-GR effective dynamics in the canonical ontology do not
supply these substrate selector objects; reusing them here would change their
declared level and is forbidden.

\section{Scoped conclusion}

For finite prospectively declared source-side models, probability and
symmetry-respecting dynamics do not create a non-fixed deterministic selector
for free.  Invariant laws form the orbit simplex, equivariant decoding returns
only fixed points, invariant inputs remain invariant, and unique global point
attractors are fixed.  Conditional branch selection remains possible through
an existing fixed point or separately sourced asymmetry and history.

This is a theorem-level finite scoped obstruction, not a no-go theorem for
all stochastic dynamics, infinite-volume phases, continuous groups,
metastability, or future source extensions.  Actual substrate dynamics remain
unconstructed and are deferred to the registered plan's later P5 construction
phase.  Adoption remains blocked while continuation is open; P3-T05 is not
executed by this artifact.
