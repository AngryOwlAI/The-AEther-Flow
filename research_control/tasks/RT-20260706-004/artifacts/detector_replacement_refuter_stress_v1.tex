\section{Stress Lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Finite/local source perturbation survival or bottom]
Under a finite source-signature-preserving relabeling or restriction of
\(\EStar\), \(\DSrc\) survives only when the relabeled or restricted records,
readout tokens, source certificates, and fail-closed branches are carried
along as source-side data. If a mutated record is not in the declared source
domain and no source-side update is supplied, the candidate returns
\(\BottomSrc\) or an explicit missing-domain obstruction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The candidate has a finite source record domain and a finite readout graph.
A source-signature-preserving relabeling changes names but not the source-side
typing of records, tokens, certificates, or fail-closed branches. A
restriction can remove graph entries and yields only a smaller finite
draft/control subcase. A new, collapsed, empirical, target, or process-repaired
record is not in the declared source domain. Since the candidate is a finite
source-side tuple, no target geometry or process receipt can extend its
domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Readout erasure blocks the positive branch]
If \(\ReadD\) is erased while the other candidate fields remain unchanged,
the positive detector-replacement branch is blocked.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\(\ReadD\) is the candidate's declared map from source records to source
readout tokens. Without that map, the tuple no longer supplies the required
readout interface. The candidate contains no alternative empirical detector
protocol, target metric, proper-time clock, process receipt, or generated
derivative that can replace \(\ReadD\). Therefore erasure fails closed instead
of producing detector semantics.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Empirical, proper-time, and target-metric substitutions fail closed]
Replacing the source readout or normalization by empirical detector protocol,
proper time, or a target metric does not preserve the candidate.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The fail-closed family includes branches for empirical import, proper-time
import, and target import. Empirical protocol data, proper-time data, target
topology, target atlas, Lorentzian metric, and benchmark behavior are outside
the source-side domain and codomain. They are not source certificates. They
therefore route to \(\BottomSrc\) rather than repairing the candidate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Placeholder survival is not detector-semantics adoption]
The status \(\texttt{supplied\_source\_placeholder}\) can survive stress only
as a draft/control candidate status, not as detector semantics.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The status labels the existence of a bounded source-side interface candidate
for audit and stress. It is explicitly not detector semantics, not empirical
measurement theory, not source-law adoption, not coupling-law adoption, and
not matter-coupling derivation. Treating the status as adoption is the
placeholder-collapse branch and is blocked.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Overread pressure does not produce matter coupling]
Stress survival of \(\DSrc\) does not supply source-law adoption, detector
semantics, coupling-law adoption, matter-coupling derivation, stress-energy
semantics, matter action, Einstein equations, benchmark promotion, or proof
authority.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The candidate contains finite source records, finite source readout tokens,
finite source certificates, fail-closed branches, and a placeholder status.
It contains no unrestricted detector law, no empirical detector protocol, no
proper-time standard, no target metric premise, no stress-energy tensor, no
matter action, no field equation, no benchmark verdict, and no Gate Chair
closure. Stress survival therefore narrows the candidate's local failure
surface but does not promote it into a physical detector theory or a

\end{proof}
