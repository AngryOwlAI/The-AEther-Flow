\section{No-Target-Import Theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Packet-interface no-target-import]
Every non-bottom certificate produced by \(\RegSold\), as defined in this
packet, depends only on \(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{src}}\) and allowed
source-extension assumptions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Each component of \(\RegSold\) has a source-domain signature:
\(\SepSrc\) reads \(\Qsrc\) and source context, \(\TopRegSrc\) reads source
support and source transition data, \(\ChartSuppSrc\) reads chart names and
source supports, \(\RegSrc\) reads source chart candidates and source
transition tokens, \(\SolderSrc\) reads \(\RespLC\) source readout tokens and
source-chart support obligations, and \(\VarRegSrc\) reads finite source
variation data. The forbidden premises are not elements of
\(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{src}}\). If any forbidden premise is used, the
\(\BottomSrc\) rule returns \(\Bot\). Therefore a non-bottom certificate
cannot depend on target topology, target atlas, target metric, empirical
semantics, benchmark success, or process authority under this packet
interface.
\end{proof}

This lemma proves only the packet-interface guard. It does not prove that
non-bottom certificates exist for arbitrary source-cover data.

If finite variation breaks regularity or soldering, route to finite-variation
robustness law refinement or scoped obstruction.
\end{branch}

If the law works only on finite or local cases, route to Candidate Constructor
finite witness. Do not claim adoption.
\end{branch}

If the law cannot be source-defined, route to scoped no-go refinement or route
freeze. Do not claim future source-extension impossibility without a separate
no-go theorem.
\end{branch}
