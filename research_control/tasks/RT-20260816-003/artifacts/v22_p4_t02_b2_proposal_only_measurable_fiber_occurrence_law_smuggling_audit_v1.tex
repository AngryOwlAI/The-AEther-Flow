\section{Coupling nonuniqueness}

Consider a section whose image contains both tokens. Let $J_0,J_1\in\{0,1\}$
indicate whether the respective token class stays fixed. Impose the common
marginal
\[
  \mathbb P(J_0=1)=\mathbb P(J_1=1)=p
\]
and token-swap symmetry. Writing
\[
  a=\mathbb P(J_0=1,J_1=1),
\]
the full exchange-symmetric coupling is
\begin{align*}
 \mathbb P(1,1)&=a,&
 \mathbb P(1,0)&=p-a,\\
 \mathbb P(0,1)&=p-a,&
 \mathbb P(0,0)&=1-2p+a.
\end{align*}
Nonnegativity is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
  \max(0,2p-1)\le a\le p.
  \label{eq:couplinginterval}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Natural token-class coupling family]
For every $a$ in~\eqref{eq:couplinginterval}, the token-class coupling is
natural under open restriction, natural under source diffeomorphism pullback,
and equivariant under the complement swap. All members have the same
one-token marginals, while distinct values of $a$ give distinct joint laws on
mixed-token sections.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Restriction discards points but preserves the token class of every retained
point. Unused token-class drivers are marginalized, so the induced law agrees
with the law assigned after restriction. Source diffeomorphisms transport the
token-valued section without changing the exchange-symmetric driver law.
Complement exchanges the identically distributed labels $0$ and $1$.
Finally, the mass of $(J_0,J_1)=(1,1)$ equals $a$, so distinct $a$ values are
separated whenever both token classes occur.
\end{proof}

The shared-global flip is the endpoint $a=p$. Independent token-class drivers
give $a=p^2$. Hence naturality and all one-token marginals do not select the
shared law.

\section{Parameter and stationarity nonselection}

For a probability measure $\mu$ on the finite complement orbits, the induced
one-step transformation is
\[
 T_p\mu=p\mu+(1-p)c_*\mu.
\]
Therefore
\[
 T_p\mu-\mu=(1-p)(c_*\mu-\mu).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Stationary-law classification]
For $p\in(0,1)$, $\mu$ is stationary under $K_p$ if and only if
$c_*\mu=\mu$. The stationary class is independent of $p$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The displayed factor $1-p$ is nonzero, so $T_p\mu=\mu$ is equivalent to
$c_*\mu=\mu$.
\end{proof}

In particular, $p=1/3$ and $p=2/3$ define distinct kernels while sharing the
uniform stationary law on every two-point complement orbit. Since $K_p$ does
not move between distinct complement orbits, arbitrary weights among those
orbits also remain stationary. Normalization, equivariance, and stationarity
therefore select neither $p$, a token, nor an orbit weight.
