\section{Pair-groupoid calculation}

Let $P=\{0,1\}$ and
\[
 G=P\times P,
 \quad s(i,j)=i,\quad t(i,j)=j,\quad
 \mathrm{id}(i)=(i,i),
\]
with $(i,j)\circ(j,k)=(i,k)$ and $(i,j)^{-1}=(j,i)$.

\begin{theorem}[Complete category and automorphism control]
$G\rightrightarrows P$ is the pair groupoid.  Its object-induced
automorphism group is $S_2=\{\mathrm{id},\tau\}$, where
$\tau(0)=1$ and $\tau(1)=0$.  These are all automorphisms and both preserve
source, target, identities, composition, and inverses.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoint and inverse equations follow immediately from ordered pairs.
For every composable triple,
\[
 ((i,j)\circ(j,k))\circ(k,l)=(i,l)
 =(i,j)\circ((j,k)\circ(k,l)),
\]
so composition is associative; the displayed identities are two-sided.
Every object permutation uniquely induces $(i,j)\mapsto(\pi i,\pi j)$, and
every groupoid automorphism restricts to an object permutation.  A two-element
set has exactly the identity and swap permutations.
\end{proof}

Take $T_f=\mathrm{id}_{\Bool}$ for every arrow.  The four Boolean predicates
on $P$ are
\[
 (0,0),\ (0,1),\ (1,0),\ (1,1),
\]
where $1$ denotes admitted.

\begin{theorem}[Total naturality classification]
A predicate $a:P\to\Bool$ is natural along every arrow of $G$ exactly when
$a(0)=a(1)$.  Therefore the only natural predicates are constant false and
constant true, and no natural predicate uniquely admits one object.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The two identity squares commute automatically.  The two nonidentity arrows
$(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$ each impose $a(0)=a(1)$ under trivial transport.  The two
constant predicates satisfy both equalities; the two remaining predicates do
not.  This exhausts all four functions $P\to\Bool$ and all four arrows.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No natural root on the unbroken torsor]

two-object control.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
