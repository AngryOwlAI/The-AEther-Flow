\subsection{Three equivariant line families}

\paragraph{Proposition (equivariant-line nonuniqueness).}

\(c(\alpha,\beta)=(\beta,\alpha)\).  There are exactly three nonzero proper
lines,
\[
 L_p=\langle p\rangle,\qquad
 L_q=\langle q\rangle,\qquad
 L_d=\langle p+q\rangle.
\]

\[

\]
defines a \(C_2\)-equivariant line family.  These three families descend to
three distinct nonzero lines in the associated four-state space.

\paragraph{Proof.}
An equivariant family is uniquely determined by its line over one orientation.
The two-dimensional \(\mathbb F_2\) space has exactly three nonzero vectors and
therefore exactly three nonzero proper lines.  Transport by \(c\) supplies the
other fibre.  Under one temporary trivialization, the descended line pulls
back to the selected base line, so distinct base lines give distinct descended
lines. \(\square\)

\(L_p,L_q\) and the invariant diagonal family \(L_d,L_d\) satisfy the same
equivariance and quotient-descent pattern.  Consequently equivariance,
