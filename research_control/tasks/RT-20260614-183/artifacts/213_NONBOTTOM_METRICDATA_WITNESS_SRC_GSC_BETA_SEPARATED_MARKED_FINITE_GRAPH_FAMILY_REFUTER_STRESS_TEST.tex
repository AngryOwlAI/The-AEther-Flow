\section{Marked-Presentation Separation}

\begin{lemma}[Marked-presentation separation for \(\BetaGraph\)]
Let \(G\) be an unmarked finite acyclic source graph. Let \(\beta_1\) and
\(\beta_2\) be incompatible admissible injective markings on \(G\). Then
\[
  \Witness(G^{\beta_1}) \quad\text{and}\quad \Witness(G^{\beta_2})
\]
are candidates on distinct marked source presentations, not two values of a
unique witness law on unmarked \(G\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The candidate's domain is not \(G\) alone. It is \(G^\beta\in\BetaGraph\),
where \(\beta\) is part of the source presentation. If \(\beta_1\) and
\(\beta_2\) are incompatible, the source presentations differ in a required
component. Forgetting \(\beta\) removes the domain condition and returns
\(\BottomSrc\) or preserves the prior missing-discriminator obstruction.
\end{proof}
