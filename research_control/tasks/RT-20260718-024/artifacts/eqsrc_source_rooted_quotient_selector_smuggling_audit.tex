\begin{lemma}[Finite quotient-selector factorization]

\section{Arbitrary-Label and Opaque-Lookup Audit}

\begin{proposition}[Primitive-profile nonuniqueness certificate]
Keep the four source tokens, \(G=\mathbb Z_4\), and \(H=\{0,2\}\) fixed.
Among the \(24\) bijective profiles \(s:\Fsrc\to G\), the quotient selector
realizes all three perfect matchings of four objects.  Each matching is
realized by eight profiles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A profile induces the unordered pair of two-element fibers of
\(q\circ s:\Fsrc\to G/H\).  Choose which two objects map to the even coset:
there are \(\binom42=6\) choices, and identifying a choice with its complement
leaves \(3\) unordered matchings.  For a fixed matching, either block may map
to the even coset; the two objects in each block may be assigned to its two
group elements in either order.  Hence \(2\cdot2\cdot2=8\) profiles realize
each matching, accounting for all \(3\cdot8=24\) bijections.
\end{proof}

This certificate shows precisely what the notation cannot establish.  The
same source tokens and the same group data support multiple inequivalent
pairings.  The selected table \(s(A_i)=i\) chooses one pairing as primitive
data.  It is not an illicit hidden lookup because it is displayed and labeled
proposal-only, but it is an unselected lookup table in the explanatory sense:
no source law derives why that table, rather than another, is physically
privileged.

\begin{lemma}[Bare-set symmetry obstruction]
An equivalence relation on a bare four-element set that is invariant under
every permutation is either equality or the universal relation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If no distinct pair is related, the relation is equality.  If one distinct
pair is related, the two-transitive action of the symmetric group sends that
ordered pair to every other ordered distinct pair.  Invariance then relates
every distinct pair, so the relation is universal.
\end{proof}

Thus a nondegenerate two-plus-two partition cannot be selected naturally from
the bare object set alone.  It needs the profile \(s\) or equivalent
symmetry-breaking source structure.  Transporting that structure under
relabeling proves equivalence of decorated presentations; it does not remove
the selection burden.

Once \(G=\mathbb Z_4\) is stipulated, \(H=\{0,2\}\) is the unique subgroup of
order two and is characteristic.  This reduces subgroup ambiguity conditional
on \(G\) and the demand for a nontrivial proper quotient.  It does not select
\(G\), the profile table, or the physical interpretation of either.

\section{Variation-Class and Stabilizer Audit}

\begin{proposition}[Exact formal stabilizer]
Let \(r:\Fsrc\to G\) and \(s_r=s+r\).  The full pairwise selector matrix is
unchanged if and only if all \(q(r(A))\) have one common value in \(G/H\).
The full additive stabilizer therefore has \(32\) elements, while
\(\Vary=H^{\Fsrc}\) is its \(16\)-element zero-coset subfamily.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(c=q\circ s:\Fsrc\to\mathbb Z_2\) and
\(d=q\circ r:\Fsrc\to\mathbb Z_2\).  The original and varied selectors are
the equality kernels of \(c\) and \(c+d\).  Because \(c\) is surjective onto
two labels, the two equality kernels agree exactly when \(c+d=c\) or
\(c+d=1-c\).  Equivalently, \(d\) is constant.  There are two choices for its
common quotient value and, for each object, two representatives of that
coset, giving \(2\cdot2^4=32\) variations.  Requiring every \(r(A)\in H\)
selects the \(d=0\) half, of size \(2^4=16\).
\end{proof}

The candidate's \(\Vary\)-stability theorem is therefore correct, as is its
qualification that \(\Vary\) is sufficient but not maximal.  Its displayed
outside-stabilizer variation changes the relation and correctly witnesses
sharpness.  However, the word ``admissible'' is a primitive designation.
Neither formal invariance nor the count \(16\) supplies a physical criterion
that selects \(\Vary\) over the \(32\)-element stabilizer, a smaller class, or
another source-side perturbation family.  The result is relative mathematical
stability, not physical admissibility or arbitrary finite-variation
robustness.

\section{Naturality and Covariance Audit}

Simultaneously transporting the source objects, profile, endpoint-indexed
fibers, and constructors along a bijection preserves the selector by
definition.  Likewise, a group automorphism preserving \(H\) preserves
subgroup membership.  These are correct equivariance statements for
transported data.

They do not choose the original profile.  In fact, transporting any one of
the three matchings produces an isomorphic selector presentation.  The
naturality statement therefore proves representation independence under its
specified transports, not physical covariance, uniqueness, observer
independence, empirical invariance, or current-ontology necessity.
