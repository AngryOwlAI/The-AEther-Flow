\begin{abstract}
This draft/control artifact audits the \texttt{RT-20260614-020}
ledger-complete source-family generator packet. The audit checks whether
the declared \(\ObjLC\), \(\GenLC\), \(\CertLC\), and \(\EqLC\) obtain
their local certificate-relation status by importing target metric,
target proper time, target locality, target observer protocol, benchmark
rank, candidate performance, validation status, or authority labels. The
result is a local conditional \(\Pass\) only for the packet as a
source-only audit object. The audit does not discharge general
\(\EqSrc\), \(\RetainH\), or \(\GenH\), does not authorize candidate
reconstruction, does not edit canonical ontology, does not promote
benchmark status, does not request Gate Chair review, and does not
permit completed-derivation language.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and Inspected Packet}

This audit responds to \texttt{handoff-0062}. The inspected packet is
\texttt{61\_LOCALIZATION\_SOURCE\_BASIS\_AXIOM\_SELECTOR\_DOMAIN\_EQSRC\_LEDGER\_COMPLETE\_SOURCE\_FAMILY\_GENERATOR\_PACKET.tex}.
The packet defines ledger atoms, a ledger-complete source-object domain
\(\ObjLC\), a source-family generator \(\GenLC\), reversible ledger
certificates \(\CertLC\), and the certificate relation \(\EqLC\). It
also states relation properties for \(\EqLC\) only on the declared
generated domain.

The audit question is:
\[
    \text{Does the packet obtain its local }\EqLC\text{ status by
    importing target success or authority labels?}
\]

The answer is no under the stated source-only constraints. The audit
therefore records a local conditional pass for the packet as a
draft/control audit object. This is not a general \(\EqSrc\) theorem.

\section{Screen Ledger}

\begin{screen}[Generator design]
The generator \(\GenLC\) is specified through source-ledger operations:
seed inclusion of registered finite source objects with available
ledgers, full source-ledger transport, branch refinement, quotient
refinement, and certified contraction transport. The audit finds no
target metric, proper time, locality region, observer protocol,
benchmark rank, or candidate-performance selector in the positive
definition of those operations.

The pass is conditional on the phrase ``full ledger'' being interpreted
literally: annotation, proxy guards, forbidden-channel guards, blockers,
influence records, quotient data, contraction data, and boundary anchors
must all be present and transported together. A generator step that uses
downstream behavior to choose an object, branch split, quotient fiber, or
representative returns \(\Block\).
\end{screen}

\begin{screen}[Ledger completeness]
The inspected packet makes ledger completeness a membership condition
for \(\ObjLC\). Objects with deleted guards, erased blockers, partial
influence coverage, moved boundary anchors, quotient data selected by
downstream behavior, or branch labels interpreted as target semantics are
excluded. The audit finds that this exclusion blocks the main local
laundering route: partial ledgers cannot be counted as positive source
objects merely because they help a downstream comparison.

Residual limitation: the packet does not provide an independent
enumeration theorem for every relevant source family. Ledger completeness
is a well-formedness screen for this constructed domain, not coverage
evidence for general \(\EqSrc\).
\end{screen}

\begin{screen}[Certificate reversibility and composition]
The relation-property proof for \(\EqLC\) rests on certificates that are
declared reversible and closed under concatenation. The audit does not
detect target import in this proof: reflexivity uses the empty
certificate, symmetry reverses source-side steps, and transitivity
concatenates source-side steps.

The proof is structurally local. It shows that the declared certificate
relation is an equivalence relation if \(\CertLC\) contains only valid
reversible source-ledger steps and remains closed under composition. It
does not show that all scientifically relevant source objects are in
\(\ObjLC\), that every intended source equivalence has a certificate, or
that certificates preserve downstream structures needed for
\(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), or candidate reconstruction.
\end{screen}

\begin{screen}[Quotient selection]
The packet permits quotient refinement only by source certificate and
rejects representative choice by downstream behavior. The audit finds no
positive use of target-favorable quotient selection. The quotient data
are treated as ledger coordinates, not as benchmark-rank knobs.

Any quotient step that deletes blockers, weakens guards, moves boundary
anchors, collapses labels into target semantics, or selects a
representative because a target comparison improves returns \(\Block\).
\end{screen}

\begin{screen}[Tri-family embedding]
The packet embeds the previously audited and stress-tested
\(T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\) tri-family result only as a subcase of \(\ObjLC\).
The audit finds no language that converts the local \(\EqTri\) receipt
into a general theorem. The embedding is a consistency check and audit
object, not promotion evidence.
\end{screen}

\begin{screen}[Validation and authority labels]
The inspected packet uses draft/control, registered-source, and
audit-eligible labels as control-state labels. Registration, bootstrap
success, generated wiki visibility, and handoff status remain evidence
of transaction hygiene, not physics evidence.

Any reading that treats validation status, a registry row, a generated
wiki page, or a handoff as canonical \(\EqSrc\), \(\RetainH\),
\(\GenH\), candidate-readiness, benchmark promotion, or Gate Chair
approval returns \(\Block\).
\end{screen}

\begin{screen}[Downstream-use language]
The packet explicitly blocks downstream bridge claims, \(\RetainH\),
\(\GenH\), candidate reconstruction, canonical ontology edit, benchmark
promotion, Gate Chair review, and completed-derivation language. The
audit finds these blocks aligned with the active claim boundary.
\end{screen}

\section{Audit Findings}

\begin{auditfinding}[No target primitive import detected locally]
Inside the declared generator packet, the positive relation status uses
ledger atoms, source labels, annotations, proxy guards,
forbidden-channel guards, blockers, influence records, quotient data,
contraction certificates, and boundary anchors. The audit finds no use
of target metric, target proper time, target locality, target observer
protocol, benchmark rank, or candidate performance as a source
primitive.
\end{auditfinding}

\begin{auditfinding}[No authority-label laundering detected]
The packet's \(\Pass\), relation-property, audit-eligible, and
draft/control labels do not create canonical ontology authority,
benchmark status, candidate-readiness, or Gate Chair authority. They
identify a local control artifact and a future stress-test object.
\end{auditfinding}

\begin{auditfinding}[Certificate relation is local by construction]
The audit accepts the relation-property proof as local to the declared
certificate relation on \(\ObjLC\). This is a formal property of the
declared reversible certificate system, not evidence that general
\(\EqSrc\) has been supplied.
\end{auditfinding}

\begin{residualrisk}[Generator coverage remains unproven]
The packet does not prove that \(\GenLC\) covers every source family
needed for the broader research program. It creates a ledger-complete
domain candidate and certificate relation for testing.
\end{residualrisk}

\begin{residualrisk}[Certificate admissibility remains stress-testable]
The packet depends on the admissibility of source-side reversible
certificate steps. A Refuter should pressure inverse validity,
composition closure, non-chain refinement, contraction transport,
guard weakening, blocker erasure, influence deletion, quotient
variation, and boundary-anchor perturbation.
\end{residualrisk}

\begin{residualrisk}[No downstream bridge supplied]
The audit does not find smuggling in the local packet, but it also does
not supply a bridge from \(\EqLC\) to \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), or
candidate reconstruction. Those claims remain blocked.
\end{residualrisk}

\section{Verdicts}

\begin{verdict}[Local conditional pass]
The \texttt{RT-20260614-020} packet conditionally passes Smuggling
Auditor review only as a source-only draft/control audit object for the
declared \(\ObjLC\), \(\GenLC\), \(\CertLC\), and \(\EqLC\) surfaces.
\end{verdict}

\begin{verdict}[Blocked overreads]
The audit blocks any overread from local \(\EqLC\) to general
\(\EqSrc\), \(\RetainH\), \(\GenH\), downstream bridge claims,
candidate reconstruction, canonical ontology edit, benchmark promotion,
Gate Chair review, or completed-derivation language.
\end{verdict}

weakening, blocker erasure, influence deletion, quotient variation,
