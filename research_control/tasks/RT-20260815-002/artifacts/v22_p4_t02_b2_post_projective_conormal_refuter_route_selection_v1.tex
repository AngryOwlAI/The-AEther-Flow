\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]

Let $F=\{0,1\}$ and let the nontrivial automorphism $g$ exchange the two
points.

\begin{proposition}[Swap-fiber trichotomy]
The action has no deterministic fixed section and no proper nonempty
invariant subset.  It has exactly one normalized invariant probability,
namely $K=(1/2,1/2)$, and that probability selects neither token.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A fixed token would satisfy $g(i)=i$, which is false for both $i=0,1$.
The only subsets preserved by the swap are $\varnothing$ and $F$.  For a
probability $(p,1-p)$, invariance gives $p=1-p$, hence $p=1/2$.  Equal weight
does not distinguish or realize either point.
\end{proof}

This result does not by itself prove a general occurrence theorem.  It is an
exact control showing why a future packet must not identify fixed-point

\subsection{A translation torsor can lack a probability kernel}

\begin{proposition}[No translation-invariant Borel probability]
There is no normalized, countably additive, translation-invariant Borel
probability measure on $\mathbb R$ under its translation action.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $m=\mu([0,1))$.  The integer translates are pairwise disjoint and have
equal mass.  If $m>0$, a sufficiently large finite union has mass exceeding
one.  Hence $m=0$.  Countable additivity then gives
$\mu(\mathbb R)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}0=0$, contradicting normalization.
\end{proof}

Thus probabilistic occurrence is not automatically available when a
deterministic section fails.  The future packet must state its measurable
spaces, action class, normalization, and additivity hypotheses exactly.
