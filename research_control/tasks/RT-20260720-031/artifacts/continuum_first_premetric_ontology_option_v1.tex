\section{New mathematical payload: normalization underdetermination}

\begin{theorem}[Order invariance does not fix metric normalization]
Let $\mathfrak O_{\mathrm{CF}}^{\mathrm{pre}}$ be a proposal instance. Suppose,
in addition to that premetric tuple, that a smooth Lorentzian metric $g$ on
$\A$ exists and satisfies
\[
   \Phi_\lambda^{*}g=g\qquad\text{for every }\lambda\in\Lambda.
\]
If no independent scale-selection law is added, then Lorentzian signature and
order invariance do not uniquely normalize the metric tensor: for every
constant $c>0$, the tensor $g_c=c^2g$ is Lorentzian and
$\Phi_\lambda$-invariant, and $g_c\ne g_d$ whenever $c\ne d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplication of a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form by the positive
number $c^2$ preserves nondegeneracy and signature, so $g_c$ is Lorentzian.
Pullback is linear, hence
\[
  \Phi_\lambda^*g_c
  =\Phi_\lambda^*(c^2g)
  =c^2\Phi_\lambda^*g
  =c^2g=g_c.
\]
If $c\ne d$, then $c^2\ne d^2$. Since a Lorentzian metric is not the zero
tensor, there are $p\in\A$ and $v,w\in T_p\A$ with
$g_p(v,w)\ne0$, so $(g_c)_p(v,w)\ne(g_d)_p(v,w)$. Thus the stated
conditions admit a continuum of distinct tensor normalizations.
\end{proof}

The theorem is intentionally narrow. It does not prove that any Lorentzian
metric exists, that nonconstant conformal freedom survives, that constant
rescalings are physically inequivalent, or that a future source-side law
cannot select a scale. It proves only that the continuum-first primitives plus
the two added properties ``Lorentzian'' and ``order invariant'' do not by
themselves supply a unique normalized metric or an operational clock scale.
