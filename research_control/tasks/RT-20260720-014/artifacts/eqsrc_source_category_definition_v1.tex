\section{Four layers that must remain distinct}

Fix a target-neutral, many-sorted signature
\(\Sigma=(\mathsf{Sort},\mathsf{Fun},\mathsf{Rel},\mathsf{Type})\). The
following layers are distinct.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(L\arabic*)]
  \item The registered canonical ontology states what source structure is
  currently authorized.
  \item A proposal-only \(\Sigma\)-record schema states mathematical fields,
  admission predicates, and provenance obligations that may exceed current
  ontology.
  \item A semantic model interprets the schema. A presentation gives names to
  its elements and fields.
  \item A serialization stores a presentation in a file. Whitespace, key
  order, file path, registry order, timestamp, validator state, and task state
  are not scientific objects or arrows.
\end{enumerate}

Loading a root, line, orientation, partition, action, grading, measure,
observer choice, or selector-relevant mark into layer L2 or L3 does not derive
it from L1. Two serializations may encode one semantic model without defining
a structural arrow or a physical equivalence.

\section{The proposed source category}

\begin{definition}[Admitted source record]
Let \(\mathsf{Adm}_{\Sigma}(X)\) be a predicate on \(\Sigma\)-structures whose
inputs are fixed source fields and source-provenance data only. An object of
\(\mathcal C_{\Sigma}\) is a pair \((X,\alpha_X)\), where \(X\) is a small
\(\Sigma\)-structure and \(\alpha_X\) is an admission certificate. The
predicate and certificate are evaluated before any eligible choice, selected
choice, induced equivalence relation, target geometry, or desired benchmark
outcome is inspected.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Candidate-blind structural morphism]
A morphism \(f:(X,\alpha_X)\to(Y,\alpha_Y)\) is a family of typed functions
\(f_s:X_s\to Y_s\), one for each source sort, together with a source-only
admissibility receipt \(\rho_f\), such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(M\arabic*)]
  \item every declared operation is preserved exactly;
  \item every declared relation is preserved in the direction specified by
  the arrow profile;
  \item source provenance required by the arrow profile is preserved; and
  \item the arrow profile and \(\rho_f\) are fixed without reference to an
  eligible or preferred choice, a \(K\)-image, target-side data, or process
  metadata.
\end{enumerate}
Arrow profiles may distinguish homomorphisms, embeddings, quotients, and
isomorphisms. A composite receives only the weakest profile justified by both
factors; no stronger profile is inferred from a desired result.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Category closure]
If the admitted arrow profiles contain the typed identity on every object and
are closed under componentwise function composition and receipt composition,
then the admitted records and morphisms form a category
\(\mathcal C_{\Sigma}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Typed identity functions preserve every operation, relation, and provenance
field and satisfy the declared identity receipt. Componentwise composites of
typed structure-preserving maps again preserve the same fields. Function
identity and associativity give
\[
f\circ\mathrm{id}_X=f,\qquad
\mathrm{id}_Y\circ f=f,\qquad
h\circ(g\circ f)=(h\circ g)\circ f.
\]
Closure of receipt composition supplies an admitted certificate for each
composite. These are exactly the category axioms.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Certified isomorphism and automorphism]
An isomorphism \(u:X\to Y\) is an admitted morphism accompanied by an explicit
inverse-provenance token
\[
\iota_u=(u^{-1},c_{\rm fwd},c_{\rm inv},c_L,c_R,r_{\rm adm}),
\]
where the two maps pass the declared structure checks,
\(c_L\) verifies \(u^{-1}u=\mathrm{id}_X\), \(c_R\) verifies
\(uu^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_Y\), and \(r_{\rm adm}\) identifies the candidate-blind
admission rule. The token records exact source and target identities and has
empty target-input fields. It is audit evidence for the inverse, not
independent scientific authority.

The core \(\mathcal G_{\Sigma}=\Core(\mathcal C_{\Sigma})\) has all objects and
only certified isomorphisms. A structural automorphism is an element of
\(\Aut_{\mathcal C_{\Sigma}}(X)=\mathcal G_{\Sigma}(X,X)\).
\end{definition}

\section{Carrier, eligible choices, and relation interface}

Let \(A:\mathcal C_{\Sigma}\to\Set\) be a declared carrier functor. On the
core, let \(D:\mathcal G_{\Sigma}\to\Set\) be a candidate-universe functor and
let \(\chi_X\) be a candidate-blind eligibility predicate satisfying
\[
\chi_X(d)\Longleftrightarrow
\chi_Y(D(u)d)
\quad\text{for every }u:X\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}Y.
\]
Define
\[
S_{\chi}(X)=\{d\in D(X):\chi_X(d)\}.
\]
The symbol \(S_{\chi}(X)\) denotes eligible choices only. It is not a
selector. A selector would be a natural section
\(1_{\mathcal G_{\Sigma}}\Rightarrow S_{\chi}\), whose existence or uniqueness
is P2-T02 work.

If an eligible choice induces a carrier equivalence relation, record maps
\[
K_X:S_{\chi}(X)\longrightarrow\EqRel(A(X)).
\]
On an isomorphism \(u:X\to Y\), define
\[
u_*R=(A(u)\times A(u))[R].
\]
The exact equivariance obligation is
\[
K_Y(S_{\chi}(u)d)=u_*K_X(d).
\]
This obligation may be checked without selecting one element of
\(S_{\chi}(X)\).

\begin{theorem}[Core relation-transport and action functoriality]
Assume \(\mathcal C_{\Sigma}\) is a category and
\(A:\mathcal C_{\Sigma}\to\Set\) is a functor. Define
\(E_A(X)=\EqRel(A(X))\) and, for \(u\) in
\(\mathcal G_{\Sigma}\), define \(E_A(u)(R)=u_*R\). Then
\(\mathcal G_{\Sigma}\) is a groupoid and
\[
E_A:\mathcal G_{\Sigma}\longrightarrow\Set
\]
is a functor. Consequently, \(\Aut(X)\) acts on \(A(X)\), on
\(E_A(X)\), and on every functorially supplied \(S_{\chi}(X)\). A family
\(K_X\) satisfying the displayed equivariance equation is precisely a natural
transformation \(S_{\chi}\Rightarrow E_A\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Identities and composites of isomorphisms are isomorphisms, and the categorical
inverse is unique, so the core is a groupoid. Since \(A(u)\) is bijective with
inverse \(A(u^{-1})\), image under \(A(u)\times A(u)\) preserves reflexivity,
symmetry, and transitivity. Functoriality of \(A\) gives
\[
(\mathrm{id}_X)_*R=R,
\qquad
(v\circ u)_*R=v_*(u_*R),
\qquad
(u^{-1})_*=(u_*)^{-1}.
\]
The group-action laws are the one-object restrictions of these functor laws.
Invariance of \(\chi\) makes \(S_{\chi}\) a subfunctor of \(D\), and the
equivariance square is exactly the naturality square for \(K\).
\end{proof}

This theorem proves only the formal category, core, relation-transport, and
action laws under stated hypotheses. It does not prove the P1-T04 natural
selector theorem, selector existence, relation uniqueness, current-ontology
derivation, physical covariance, or physical admissibility.

\section{Noninvertible arrows and variations}

For an arbitrary carrier map \(f:A(X)\to A(Y)\), equivalence relations have a
canonical contravariant pullback
\[
f^*R=\{(x,x'):(f(x),f(x'))\in R\}.
\]
There is no neutral covariant transport on all noninvertible maps. A raw direct
image can fail reflexivity on the full codomain and can fail transitivity when
images of distinct source classes overlap. Taking an equivalence closure adds
an extra rule. Any future extension of \(S_{\chi}\) or \(K\) to a
noninvertible arrow must declare exactly one of:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(N\arabic*)]
  \item preservation,
  \(K_X(d)\subseteq f^*K_Y(S(f)d)\);
  \item exact reflection,
  \(K_X(d)=f^*K_Y(S(f)d)\), which implies
  \(\ker A(f)\subseteq K_X(d)\);
  \item a proved quotient- or span-specific transport law; or
  \item \texttt{undefined}, which fails closed.
\end{enumerate}

A variation is independently declared typed change data
\(\nu:X\rightsquigarrow X'\), not automatically a structural morphism. A
variation record names changed fields, preserved fields, admission conditions,
and one outcome among \texttt{preserved}, \texttt{changed}, or
\(\bot(\texttt{tag})\). A variation category may be formed only after its own
identity and composition receipts are proved. Algebraic translations or
relabelings that preserve every line are controls, not physical perturbations.
