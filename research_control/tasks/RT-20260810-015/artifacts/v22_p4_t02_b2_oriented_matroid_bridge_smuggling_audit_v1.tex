\section{Audit standard}

``Target-free formula'' and ``source-pure candidate as written'' are not the
same judgment.  A disclosed arbitrary proposal input can be lawful for a
bounded existence witness without being current-ontology-derived.  It becomes
a repair issue when all of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item the restriction controls the stated theorem domain rather than merely
  one test fixture;
  \item the construction itself does not select the restriction;
  \item the restriction is unnecessary for the theorem;
  \item it coincides with a salient target datum; and
  \item no independent source rationale or explicit nonphysical budget label
  is supplied.
\end{enumerate}
This standard flags a target-shaped preload risk without asserting that a
target datum was deliberately imported.  The stronger
\texttt{forbidden\_target\_import} verdict requires evidence not present here.

\section{New exact payloads}

\subsection{General finite-rank extension}

Let $r\ge1$, let $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ be the standard basis of $\Q^r$, and define
\[
 A_r^+=\bigl[e_1\ \cdots\ e_r\ \ \mathbf{1}_r\bigr],\qquad
 A_r^-=\bigl[e_1\ \cdots\ e_r\ \ -\mathbf{1}_r\bigr].
\]
For $A_r^+$, the dual vector $k=\mathbf{1}_r$ is strictly positive on every
column.  Hence the feasible branch holds and no nonzero nonnegative kernel
vector exists.  For $A_r^-$,
\[
 e_1+\cdots+e_r+(-\mathbf{1}_r)=0
\]
is a strictly positive dependence on all $r+1$ columns.  Every proper subset
is independent or cannot sum positively to zero, so this is a minimal positive
circuit with support $r+1$.

The standard support argument also works for arbitrary finite $A$: a
support-minimal positive dependence has rank $|C|-1$, one-dimensional kernel,
and therefore $|C|\le r+1$.  None of the covector, alternative,
presentation, refinement, or tame-path proofs requires $r\le4$.  The exact
model verifies the family for $1\le r\le6$; at $r=5$ and $r=6$ the negative
controls produce minimal positive circuits of sizes six and seven.  Thus the
general theorem is finite-rank, while ``at most five'' is only the artifact of
the selected cap.

\subsection{Reorientation dependence}

The passage from $A_r^+$ to $A_r^-$ is independent negative reorientation of
one column.  It is not an arrow of the positive presentation groupoid and it
changes \texttt{Feasible} to \texttt{Obstructed}.  Therefore
$\Bridge(A)$ records the consequences of a supplied orientation but cannot
select or physically justify that orientation.  This is a source-input
nonselection theorem, not a defect in Gordan's alternative.

\subsection{Sector, refinement, and path nonselection}

The same basis can be refined by adjoining $+\mathbf{1}_r$ or
$-\mathbf{1}_r$, giving opposite total branches.  Both are exact declared
refinements, so $\operatorname{Ref}$ transports a chosen tower but does not
choose one.  Likewise, on $[-1,1]$ the tame polynomial $p_\times(t)=t$ has one
isolated wall while $p_0(t)=t+2$ has none.  Both are admissible path inputs;
$\operatorname{Strat}$ records but does not select the family.  These controls
show why finite conformance cannot be read as universal or physical
robustness.

\section{Decisive verdict}

The decisive verdict is \texttt{repair\_required}.  The fixed conditional
mathematics contains no hidden target metric, atlas, empirical answer, physical
causal semantics, frozen-route replay, or workflow authority.  Nevertheless,
source-purity acceptance cannot pass while the general theorem carries an
unexplained and unnecessary $r\le4$ cap that matches the target dimension.

The smallest repair is exact:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item replace the general domain by arbitrary finite rank $r\ge1$;
  \item replace $|C|\le r+1\le5$ by $|C|\le r+1$;
  \item retain rank-at-most-four examples only under the explicit label
  ``nonphysical finite conformance budget''; and
  \item preserve every other definition, theorem, typing repair, status
  boundary, and freeze.
\end{enumerate}
This verdict does not prove deliberate target import, candidate physical
invalidity, a global no-go theorem, or future source-extension impossibility.
