\section{Fixed candidate retained from RT007}

Let $V$ be four-dimensional and let $u,z\in V$ be independent.  Fix
$0<\epsilon<1$ and set
\[
 v_R=u+\epsilon z,\qquad v_S=u,\qquad v_D=u-\epsilon z.
\]
After the proposal-only typed relabelings into one bookkeeping line, define
\[
 \Resp(k)=\bigl(k(v_R),k(v_S),k(v_D)\bigr).
\]
Writing $a=k(u)$ and $b=k(z)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-response}
 \Resp(k)=(a+\epsilon b,a,a-\epsilon b).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed RT007 theorem retained]
The image of \eqref{eq:fixed-response} is the plane
\[
 r_R+r_D=2r_S,
\]
$\operatorname{rank}\Resp=2$, and
$\ker\Resp=\ann(\operatorname{span}\{u,z\})$.  On the domain where all three
responses are nonzero, the positive-projective image is described by
\[
 \sigma=\sgn(a),\qquad
 t=\epsilon b/a,\qquad
 [\Resp(k)]_+=[\sigma(1+t),\sigma,\sigma(1-t)]_+.
\]
It realizes exactly six nonzero ordered sign cells and excludes the two
alternating cells.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first and third coordinates in \eqref{eq:fixed-response} add to $2a$.
Independence of $u,z$ gives rank two and the stated annihilator kernel.  The
projective formula follows after division by $|a|$.  For $\sigma=+1$, the
intervals $t<-1$, $-1<t<1$, and $t>1$ give $-++$, $+++$, and $++-$;
the negative sheet gives their simultaneous sign reversals.  This is the
committed RT007 calculation, restated without alteration.
\end{proof}

The common-positive set is
\[

              =\{k:a>\epsilon|b|\}.
\]
The fixed product, common cone, covariance statement, and strict-margin local
witness remain valid in their declared scope.

\section{Collapse stress}

\begin{proposition}[Noncollapse is necessary]
If $\epsilon=0$ or $z=0$ while $u\ne0$, then
$v_R=v_S=v_D=u$, the response rank is one, the positive-projective image has
only the two diagonal sheets, and the nonzero sign image is
$\{+++,---\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The response becomes $(a,a,a)$.  Its image is the diagonal line and its
nonzero positive-ray quotient has the two representatives $(1,1,1)$ and
$(-1,-1,-1)$.  The common-positive set remains the nonempty half-space
$k(u)>0$.
\end{proof}

Thus the one-coordinate, six-cell classification needs an explicit
noncollapse hypothesis.  Collapse does not refute common positivity; it
refutes treating the fine quotient geometry as automatic.

\section{Nonuniqueness and inverse defect}

\begin{lemma}[Exact quotient fibers]
For the fixed candidate, every response has a two-dimensional affine covector
fiber.  A positive-ray class has the preimage
\[
 \{c k_0+h:c>0,\ h\in\ann(\operatorname{span}\{u,z\})\},
\]
and therefore has three continuous degrees of freedom.  Each nonempty sign
cell is an open full-dimensional cone in $V^*$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The kernel has dimension $4-2=2$.  Fixing a response gives one affine coset of
that kernel.  Fixing only a positive ray permits one additional positive scale.
Sign conditions are strict inequalities and hence define open subsets of the
four-dimensional cotangent space.
\end{proof}

For example, in the coordinate presentation $u=e_0,z=e_1$,
\[
 k=(1,1/2,0,0),\qquad k'=(1,1/2,9,-4)
\]
give the same response $(7/6,1,5/6)$ when $\epsilon=1/3$.  Hence neither
$\Resp$, $\Qray$, nor $\Qsign$ reconstructs a unique source covector.  They
also do not reconstruct physical normalization, empirical response semantics,
sector multiplicity, units, or coupling.

\section{Positive-line transition and cocycle stress}

The formal response values initially live in three typed one-dimensional
lines.  RT007 used fixed proposal-only isomorphisms into a common bookkeeping
line.  Let a change of those local trivializations multiply the three
coordinates by positive functions $\lambda_R,\lambda_S,\lambda_D$.

\begin{theorem}[Common positive-ray descent condition]
Independent positive response-line factors preserve $\Qsign$.  They preserve
$\Qray$ for every response in the fixed image only if
\[
 \lambda_R=\lambda_S=\lambda_D.
\]
On overlaps, the resulting common factor must additionally satisfy the usual
triple-overlap cocycle for $\Qray$ to globalize.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Positive coordinate factors cannot change signs.  The fixed image contains
the diagonal response $(1,1,1)$.  Its transformed response is
$(\lambda_R,\lambda_S,\lambda_D)$, which lies on the same positive ray as
$(1,1,1)$ exactly when all three factors agree.  A common factor is sufficient
because positive projectivization removes it.  Agreement of locally defined
rays across triple intersections then requires the common transition factors
to compose to the identity.
\end{proof}

The explicit countercontrol $(1,1,1)\mapsto(2,3,5)$ preserves the $+++$ sign
cell but changes the positive ray.  Thus $\Qray$ is valid fixed-presentation
syntax, not an intrinsic global response quotient, unless a new source-derived
common-line descent law and cocycle are supplied.  $\Qsign$ avoids positive
normalization dependence but still needs a coherent orientation convention and
does not acquire empirical or physical meaning from invariance alone.

\section{Arbitrarily small finite-variation countermodel}

Define the linear midpoint constraint
\[
 F:V^3\longrightarrow V,\qquad
 F(v_R,v_S,v_D)=v_R-2v_S+v_D.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Codimension of the split midpoint family]
The exact midpoint-law triples form $\ker F$, a codimension-four linear
subspace of $V^3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$F$ is surjective: every $x\in V$ is $F(x,0,0)$.  Hence
$\operatorname{rank}F=\dim V=4$.  Rank--nullity gives codimension four.
\end{proof}

If the sector triples are restricted by
$h_0(v_R)=h_0(v_S)=h_0(v_D)=1$, their affine tangent space is
$(\ker h_0)^3$.  The restriction of $F$ is still surjective onto
$\ker h_0$, so the normalized midpoint family has codimension three.  It too
has empty interior under arbitrary independent normalized sector variation.

Choose $w\notin\operatorname{span}\{u,z\}$ and, for any nonzero $\delta$, set
\[
 v_R^{(\delta)}=u+\epsilon z+\delta w,\qquad
 v_S^{(\delta)}=u,\qquad
 v_D^{(\delta)}=u-\epsilon z.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Arbitrarily small transverse rank jump]
For every $\delta\ne0$, the perturbed response map has rank three and is
surjective onto $\RR^3$.  It realizes all eight nonzero ordered sign cells.
Nevertheless the covector satisfying
$k(u)=1$, $k(z)=k(w)=0$ remains strictly common positive.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the basis $u=e_0,z=e_1,w=e_2$, a three-by-three minor of the response-vector
matrix is
\[
 \det\begin{pmatrix}
 1&\epsilon&\delta\\
 1&0&0\\
 1&-\epsilon&0
 \end{pmatrix}=-\epsilon\delta\ne0.
\]
Thus the response rank is three.  For an arbitrary target response
$(x,y,\zeta)$ choose
\[
 a=y,\qquad b=\frac{y-\zeta}{\epsilon},\qquad
 c=\frac{x-2y+\zeta}{\delta};
\]
the covector with $k(u)=a,k(z)=b,k(w)=c$ realizes that response.  Choosing
$x,y,\zeta\in\{\pm1\}$ realizes all eight sign cells.  The stated
common-positive covector evaluates to $(1,1,1)$ for every $\delta$.
\end{proof}

Since $\delta$ may be arbitrarily small, no open neighborhood in the full
independent-sector product $V^3$ preserves the rank-two plane or six-cell
image.  Yet common positivity remains open.  This is the decisive separation:
the candidate's strict common-positive witness is robust, while its fine
quotient geometry is robust only inside a declared structured variation class.

\begin{corollary}[Common-positive fine-quotient irrelevance]
For the sole property $k\in\Gamma_\cap$, the continuous coordinate of $\Qray$
and the five nonpositive sign cells carry no additional decision information.

\end{corollary}

This corollary does not prove that every possible bridge use of $\Qray$ is
irrelevant.  It creates one honest continuation option: a future packet may
