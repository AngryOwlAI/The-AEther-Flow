\section{Stress Verdict}

\begin{theorem}[Fail-closed certificate stress theorem]
Let \(\Asrc\) and \(\Bsrc\) be declared finite/local source-side
matter-semantics objects in the P2 theorem domain. Under the P2-T05 stress
family, every branch has one of the following outcomes:
\[
  \Asrc \EqMScert \Bsrc
  \quad \text{under explicit valid source certificates,}
\]
\[
  \WitnessId
  \quad \text{when a required certificate is removed, malformed, or mismatched,}
\]
or a blocked overread when the branch uses target metric, detector semantics,
\(\RRE\) collapse, benchmark behavior, process authority, scoped evidence, or
source-extension status as equivalence authority. No branch yields source-law
adoption, matter-semantics adoption, detector-semantics adoption, matter
coupling, stress-energy semantics, matter action, Einstein equations,
benchmark promotion, completed derivation, or program-wide no-go authority.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The P2-T03 theorem is conditional. Its positive conclusion depends on explicit
source transport, source invariance, source factorization, and no-target
guard certificates. If those certificates are present and well formed, the
Refuter stress does not produce a contradiction in the theorem as written.
If one of the required certificates is removed, malformed, or attached to the
wrong source object, the smallest failed premise is the explicit valid
source-certificate premise. The equivalence claim then has no lawful source
authority and fails closed. Branches that replace the missing certificate by a
target metric, detector semantics, \(\RRE\) collapse, benchmark behavior,
generated output, registry state, validation state, approval state, or
source-extension status are outside the theorem language and therefore
blocked rather than promoted. Thus the conditional theorem survives while
unconditional or adoption-style readings fail by a finite/local witness.
\end{proof}

\section{Pressure Lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Certificate deletion guard]
If a proof of \(\Asrc \EqMScert \Bsrc\) depends on an explicit source
transport, source invariance, or source factorization certificate, then
deleting that certificate invalidates the proof branch. The result is the
certificate-gap witness, not detector semantics or target geometry.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The positive branch has no certificate-free premise. Removing the named
certificate leaves no source-side witness from which equivalence can be
derived.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Malformed-certificate guard]
If a supplied certificate has the wrong object endpoints, wrong domain,
wrong codomain, wrong relation, or wrong factorization target, then it is not
a certificate for \(\Asrc,\Bsrc\). The equivalence claim therefore fails
closed.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Certificate validity is object-indexed in the P2 manifest and theorem. A
certificate for another pair or another comparison cannot discharge the
premise for this pair.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No detector or target repair]
A detector semantics, target metric, proper-time structure, benchmark fit,
\(\MetricData(E)\), \(\geff\), matter action, stress-energy tensor, or
Einstein-equation premise cannot repair the certificate gap unless a separate
source-side certificate is first constructed without that import.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The P2-T04 audit found the theorem source-pure as written precisely because it
does not use those target or empirical objects. Introducing them as repair
premises changes the theorem language and violates the no-target boundary.
\end{proof}
