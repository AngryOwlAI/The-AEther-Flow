# Physics Program Manuscript Outline v15

## Scope

This artifact implements v15 P18-T01. It is a control Markdown outline for a
future physics-program manuscript. It is not a submitted manuscript, not a
publication action, not a physics proof, not a canonical ontology edit, and
not authority for benchmark promotion or completed GR derivation.

The outline's governing discipline is category separation: interpretation,
model, derivation, and benchmark recovery must remain distinct. The paper may
describe the AEther-flow program as a proposed research interpretation and a
source-side modeling program. It must not present the current repository state
as a completed first-principles derivation of general relativity.

## Manuscript Thesis

Proposed thesis:

> The AEther-flow research program can be presented as a source-first
> interpretation and derivation program with exact-GR benchmark obligations,
> scoped mathematical artifacts, explicit claim gates, and preserved negative
> results. Its present value is a controlled map of what has been constructed,
> what remains scoped, and what remains open, not a completed GR derivation.

## Required Claim Boundary

The manuscript must preserve these boundary statements in the introduction or
status note:

- AEther-flow is a proposed research ontology / interpretation, not an
  established physical ontology.
- Exact-GR benchmark recovery remains a protected target and is not promoted
  by this outline.
- Scoped source-side objects and source-extension evidence are not downstream
  matter coupling, stress-energy semantics, matter action, Einstein equations,
  benchmark promotion, or completed derivation.
- Validator receipts, generated derivatives, and public status tables are
  operational or communication evidence only; they are not scientific proof.
- Negative results constrain route reuse while leaving explicitly described
  future source-extension or redesigned-route work open.

## Proposed Paper Structure

### 1. Project Motivation

Purpose: explain why the project investigates a source-first interpretation of
relativistic structure.

Content requirements:

- motivate the distinction between explanatory ontology, mathematical model,
  and physical theory;
- state the exact-GR benchmark as an obligation target rather than a completed
  recovery;
- explain why a controlled research ledger is needed for speculative
  theoretical work.

Boundary: do not claim empirical adequacy, physical ontology adoption, or
first-principles recovery.

### 2. Exact-GR Benchmark Boundary

Purpose: define exact-GR benchmark recovery as a protected benchmark burden.

Content requirements:

- identify exact-GR compatibility / recovery as a benchmark target;
- distinguish benchmark compatibility from first-principles derivation;
- state that benchmark promotion remains blocked absent tracked protected
  authority.

Boundary: no Einstein-equation derivation, benchmark promotion, or completed
  derivation language.

### 3. Proposed AEther-Flow Ontology as Research Ontology

Purpose: present the ontology as research ontology / interpretive frame.

Content requirements:

- describe source-side structures as proposed research primitives or
  source-side constructs;
- specify that source-side naming does not itself create physical semantics;
- connect the ontology to open derivation burdens rather than to established
  theory status.

Boundary: no canonical ontology edit and no established physical ontology
claim.

### 4. Authority and Claim-Boundary Method

Purpose: make the manuscript self-auditing.

Content requirements:

- explain the repository authority hierarchy: registered TeX, registries,
  registered Markdown, and generated derivatives;
- explain Gate Chair, validator receipt, and claim-language boundaries;
- state how scoped evidence/preconditions differ from adoption.

Boundary: do not treat workflow validation as physics validation.

### 5. `Resp_lc` Scoped Source-Extension Data

Purpose: summarize `Resp_lc` only within its scoped source-extension status.

Content requirements:

- identify `Resp_lc` as source-side / source-extension material under tracked
  scope;
- state the old-tuple selector obstruction where relevant;
- keep same-milestone continuation and future selector-law work open.

Boundary: do not use `Resp_lc` as detector semantics, matter coupling, or
unrestricted downstream support.

### 6. `M_src` Scoped Source-Only Object

Purpose: summarize `M_src` as a scoped source-only object.

Content requirements:

- state that `M_src` is not the target spacetime manifold;
- explain what role it can play in source-side construction;
- identify missing downstream semantics before physical-object status.

Boundary: no target-manifold, metric, matter-coupling, or GR-derivation
overread.

### 7. `g_eff` Scoped Source-Extension Object

Purpose: summarize `g_eff` as a scoped source-extension object.

Content requirements:

- distinguish source-side effective metric data from an unscoped Lorentzian
  metric claim;
- identify what remains missing before matter coupling or Einstein-equation
  work;
- state that `g_eff` status does not by itself promote benchmark recovery.

Boundary: no `g_eff` adoption, scope expansion, matter coupling, or
Einstein-equation claim.

### 8. Matter-Sector Evidence / Preconditions

Purpose: collect the current matter-sector status without promoting it.

Content requirements:

- separate matter semantics, detector semantics, coupling law, and
  stress-energy semantics;
- summarize scoped evidence/preconditions only as prerequisites or constraints;
- identify why matter coupling remains an open burden.

Boundary: evidence/precondition status is not adoption, matter coupling, or
stress-energy semantics.

### 9. `RR_E` Separation and Certificates

Purpose: explain the role of `RR_E` transport/invariance material and source
certificates.

Content requirements:

- explain why unrestricted `RR_E` readings are blocked under current premises;
- identify certificate-indexed routes as possible future constrained work;
- connect `RR_E` separation to claim-boundary and no-target-import discipline.

Boundary: no unrestricted `RR_E` theorem, source-law adoption, or detector
collapse.

### 10. Current Matter-Semantics Theorem or Obstruction

Purpose: name the current matter-semantics route status at the time of this
outline.

Content requirements:

- state that current matter-semantics work remains under controlled scoped
  theorem / obstruction discipline;
- identify conditional theorem status, certificate gaps, or obstruction status
  only when supported by tracked sources;
- frame open work as explicit next-burden resolution rather than hidden
  completion.

Boundary: do not convert theorem attempts, audits, or stress tests into
matter-coupling derivation.

### 11. Open Burdens

Purpose: provide the reader with the unresolved derivation map.

Minimum burdens to list:

- source-side law or selector status still needed for current blocked routes;
- matter semantics and detector semantics;
- coupling law;
- stress-energy semantics and matter action;
- Einstein-equation route;
- benchmark promotion;
- Gate Chair status where protected authority is required.

Boundary: open burdens must remain open unless a cited authority source closes
them.

### 12. Negative Results

Purpose: show why failed routes are useful.

Content requirements:

- explain frozen routes and scoped obstructions;
- cite representative negative-result families from the v15 inventory;
- state what each blocks and what remains open.

Boundary: no program-wide no-go theorem and no future source-extension
impossibility claim.

### 13. No Completed GR Derivation

Purpose: close with exact status discipline.

Content requirements:

- state plainly that the current project has not completed a first-principles
  GR derivation;
- explain what would be required for stronger claims;
- route future work to controlled derivation-burden packets.

Boundary: no rhetorical substitution of aspiration, compatibility, validation,
or public communication for completed derivation.
