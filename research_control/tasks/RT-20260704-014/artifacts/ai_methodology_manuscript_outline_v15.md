# AI Research-Agent Methodology Manuscript Outline v15

## Scope

This artifact implements v15 P18-T02. It is a control Markdown outline for a
future AI research-agent methodology manuscript. It is not a submitted
manuscript, not a publication action, not a physics result, not a claim that AI
agents possess independent scientific authority, and not authority for claim
promotion.

The manuscript's governing discipline is separation: AI-system claims describe
workflow design, routing controls, bounded execution, memory navigation,
validator receipts, adversarial review, and human accountability. Physics
claims remain governed by the repository authority hierarchy and cannot be
established by the AI workflow itself.

## Manuscript Thesis

Proposed thesis:

> A research-agent system can support speculative theoretical physics when it
> is organized as a bounded control workflow with explicit role contracts,
> source-backed memory navigation, adversarial review, validator receipts,
> preserved negative results, route-orbit detection, and human-gated authority.
> The method improves traceability and error resistance, but it does not grant
> autonomous scientific authority or replace formal proof, empirical evidence,
> or human accountability.

## AI-System Claims Versus Physics Claims

The paper must preserve this distinction throughout.

| Claim class | Manuscript may say | Manuscript must not say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI-system method | The workflow structures agent labor into bounded tasks with receipts. | The workflow proves a physical theory. |
| Routing | The Director role selects exactly one next bounded route from tracked state. | Route selection creates scientific truth or claim promotion. |
| Validation | Validators check schema, registry, language, and derivative consistency. | Validator PASS is scientific proof or Gate Chair adoption. |
| Memory | Memory helps locate authority sources and prior decisions. | Retrieval layers override tracked canonical sources. |
| Adversarial review | Smuggling audits and Refuter stress expose hidden assumptions and local failures. | Stress success or failure implies global theory acceptance or rejection. |
| Human gates | Protected claims require explicit human-gated authority. | AI agents may self-authorize ontology adoption or benchmark promotion. |

## Proposed Paper Structure

### 1. Motivation for AI-Scaffolded Theoretical Physics

Purpose: explain why speculative physics research benefits from controlled
agent scaffolding.

Content requirements:

- motivate the problem of long-horizon theoretical work with many provisional
  objects, failed routes, and authority boundaries;
- distinguish AI support for navigation, drafting, audit, and validation from
  AI authority over truth;
- explain why structured negative-result preservation is preferable to
  discarding failed branches.

Boundary: do not imply that AI-generated text or tool execution establishes a
physics claim.

### 2. Director-Led Routing

Purpose: describe the Director of Research as a routing-control phase.

Content requirements:

- define Director-led routing as one bounded next-route selection from tracked
  state;
- explain the use of handoffs, program state, and implementation-plan
  constraints;
- state that the Director may create one AgentJob per invocation under the
  continue-research boundary.

Boundary: Director routing is not scientific adoption, proof authority, or
benchmark authority.

### 3. Role Contracts

Purpose: present roles as scoped execution constraints.

Content requirements:

- distinguish routing-control, documentation-control, validation, scientific
  construction, adversarial, and human-gated roles;
- explain role overlays and one-job provisional roles as task-local
  constraints;
- state that roles do not expand authority unless a tracked gate permits it.

Boundary: role assignment is not claim promotion.

### 4. AgentJob Boundedness

Purpose: explain why bounded jobs reduce route drift and scope laundering.

Content requirements:

- define a bounded AgentJob by objective, allowed reads, allowed writes,
  expected outputs, validators, and stop conditions;
- explain why one outer AgentJob prevents uncontrolled multi-branch authority
  expansion;
- describe completion receipts as traceability records.

Boundary: a completed AgentJob is process evidence only.

### 5. Claim Gates

Purpose: show how the workflow prevents category upgrades.

Content requirements:

- define proof authority, support-only status, human gates, and protected
  promotion categories;
- explain blocked claims for source-law adoption, matter semantics, coupling,
  stress-energy, Einstein equations, benchmark promotion, and completed
  derivation;
- connect claim gates to public-overread prevention.

Boundary: a gate blocks or permits exact status changes only within its
declared scope.

### 6. Smuggling Audits

Purpose: present smuggling audits as hidden-assumption detection.

Content requirements:

- explain target-import, downstream-semantics, and validator-authority
  smuggling risks;
- describe audits as adversarial checks against hidden use of the desired
  result;
- show how audit artifacts preserve unresolved limitations.

Boundary: an audit PASS is not a proof of the target theory.

### 7. Refuter Stress

Purpose: explain Refuter stress as local failure discovery and route hardening.

Content requirements:

- define stress tests as attempts to collapse a claim under edge cases,
  countermodels, missing certificates, or underdetermination;
- distinguish scoped obstruction, frozen local route, and global no-go theorem;
- state that repeated stress results must preserve what remains open.

Boundary: a local failure does not authorize future source-extension
impossibility or program-wide rejection.

### 8. Gate Chair Reviews

Purpose: describe human-gated protected reviews.

Content requirements:

- identify Gate Chair review as protected authority for exact scoped status
  decisions;
- distinguish scoped evidence-status acceptance from adoption of source laws,
  physical semantics, coupling, benchmark status, or completed derivation;
- explain why user authorization must be exact about the gate scope.

Boundary: Gate Chair review cannot be generalized beyond its recorded scope.

### 9. Distance-to-GR Ledger

Purpose: show how Distance-to-GR status is tracked.

Content requirements:

- explain the ledger as a control registry of burdens, status layers, blocking
  burdens, and overread guards;
- distinguish no-distance-delta process work from scientific status changes;
- describe how exact-GR benchmark promotion remains blocked until all required
  upstream authority exists.

Boundary: a ledger row communicates tracked status; it is not an independent
derivation.

### 10. Theorem Inventory

Purpose: explain theorem inventory as a frontier map.

Content requirements:

- describe theorem-like objects, missing theorems, scoped accepted objects,
  obstructions, and frozen routes;
- distinguish inventory summary from canonical source proof;
- connect inventory rows to route selection and external review.

Boundary: inventory presence does not create a theorem.

### 11. Memory System

Purpose: describe memory as retrieval and navigation.

Content requirements:

- explain memory preflight, status checks, targeted queries, canonical source
  inspection, source hashes, and registry rows;
- state that Obsidian notes, semantic extracts, SQLite indexes, wiki notes,
  and `.local/` caches are retrieval layers only;
- show how memory reduces forgotten-context risk while preserving authority
  hierarchy.

Boundary: memory hits never override tracked sources.

### 12. Generated Derivative Boundary

Purpose: prevent generated outputs from becoming authority.

Content requirements:

- distinguish registered sources from generated wiki notes, generated indexes,
  dependency graphs, PDFs, HTML, semantic extracts, and vault mirrors;
- explain why generated derivatives are regenerated after source changes;
- state that derivative freshness supports navigation and review, not proof.

Boundary: derivative consistency is not scientific validation.

### 13. Negative-Result Preservation

Purpose: argue that failed routes are durable research assets.

Content requirements:

- define negative results as scoped route failures, countermodels,
  obstructions, or frozen local routes;
- explain what a negative result blocks and what it does not block;
- show why preserving negative results prevents circular rediscovery.

Boundary: negative-result entries do not authorize global theory rejection
unless a separate theorem and protected authority supply that claim.

### 14. Route-Orbit Detection

Purpose: describe route-cycle detection as process integrity.

Content requirements:

- define route signatures, milestone/burden repetition, payload classes,
  source-extension classifications, and freeze thresholds;
- distinguish legitimate multi-step theorem work from process repetition;
- explain why a route-orbit warning is advisory until a tracked review packet
  creates exact scope.

Boundary: route-orbit detection is not a physics conclusion.

### 15. Limitations and Human Accountability

Purpose: make the method's limits explicit.

Content requirements:

- state that language models are probabilistic systems that require grounding,
  inspection, and validation;
- identify hallucination, authority laundering, tool-error, stale-memory,
  validator-gap, and overfitting-to-workflow risks;
- assign accountability to human-gated authority for protected claims and to
  tracked source evidence for scientific status.

Boundary: the system improves process discipline only when humans maintain
authority boundaries and inspect evidence.

## Method Evaluation Plan

The manuscript can evaluate the method with process metrics, not physics
promotion metrics:

- number of bounded AgentJobs completed with receipts;
- rate of validator or claim-language failures caught before checkpoint;
- count of negative results preserved and reused;
- route-orbit warnings and reviewed outcomes;
- instances where memory hits led to canonical source inspection;
- cases where Gate Chair review changed only exact scoped status;
- examples where smuggling audits prevented target import.

These metrics should be presented as workflow evidence. They do not measure
truth of the physics program.

## Drafting Requirements

- Preserve AI-system / physics-claim separation in every section.
- Use APA 7 citations for internal documents and any external methodology
  sources selected later.
- Keep all examples source-backed and scoped.
- Do not anthropomorphize language models beyond operational role labels.
- Do not present a validator, commit, generated derivative, role output,
  memory hit, or handoff as scientific proof.
- Do not claim autonomous scientific authority.
