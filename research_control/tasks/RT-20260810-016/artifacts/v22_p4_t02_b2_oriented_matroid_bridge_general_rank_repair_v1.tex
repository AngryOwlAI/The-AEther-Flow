\section{General-rank positive-circuit theorem}

\subsection{Support bound}

\textbf{Theorem.}  For every finite configuration $A$ of rank $r$ and every
$C\in\Cirp(A)$,
\[
 \operatorname{rank}(A_C)=|C|-1\le r,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad |C|\le r+1.
\]

\textbf{Proof.}  Let $\alpha_C>0$ satisfy $A_C\alpha_C=0$.  Suppose that
$\ker A_C$ had dimension greater than one and choose
$\beta\in\ker A_C$ independent of $\alpha_C$.  Because $C$ is finite and
$\alpha_C$ is strictly positive, one can move from $\alpha_C$ along one of the
two directions $\pm\beta$ until a coordinate first reaches zero while every
coordinate remains nonnegative.  The endpoint is nonzero, lies in
$\ker A_C$, and has proper support.  This contradicts support minimality of
$C$.  Hence $\dim\ker A_C=1$ and
$\operatorname{rank}(A_C)=|C|-1$.  Since $A_C$ is a subconfiguration of $A$,
its rank is at most $r$, proving $|C|\le r+1$.  The argument contains no
absolute-rank or target-dimension premise.  \hfill$\square$

For implementation, complete circuit enumeration therefore tests supports of
size at most $r+1$ for every finite $r$.  The old printed suffix
``$\le5$'' is removed rather than generalized to another absolute ceiling.

\subsection{Sharp branch-pair family}

For each $r\ge1$, let $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ be the standard basis of $\Q^r$ and
define
\begin{align*}
 A_r^+&=[e_1,\ldots,e_r,+\textstyle\sum_i e_i],\\
 A_r^-&=[e_1,\ldots,e_r,-\textstyle\sum_i e_i].
\end{align*}
For $A_r^+$, the dual vector $k=(1,\ldots,1)$ has evaluations
$(1,\ldots,1,r)$, so the strict branch is Feasible.  Consequently the
unchanged total alternative forbids a nonzero nonnegative kernel certificate.

For $A_r^-$,
\[
 e_1+\cdots+e_r+\left(-\sum_i e_i\right)=0
\]
is a strictly positive relation on all $r+1$ columns.  Deleting the last
column leaves a basis.  Deleting $e_j$ leaves a set in which the negative-sum
column has a nonzero $j$ component outside the span of the remaining basis
vectors.  Every proper subset is therefore independent.  The displayed
circuit is unique, support-minimal, and has size $r+1$, so the bound is sharp
for every finite rank.
