\section{Natural coupling has no isolation margin}

For a section containing both token values, let $J_0,J_1\in\{0,1\}$ denote
the stay indicators of the two token classes. Exchange symmetry and common
marginal $\mathbb P(J_i=1)=p$ give the family
\begin{align}
 \mathbb P(1,1)&=a,&
 \mathbb P(1,0)&=p-a,\nonumber\\
 \mathbb P(0,1)&=p-a,&
 \mathbb P(0,0)&=1-2p+a,
 \label{eq:coupling}
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
 \max(0,2p-1)\leq a\leq p.
 \label{eq:interval}
\end{equation}
Every member is natural under open restriction and source-diffeomorphism
pullback and is token-swap equivariant, as proved in RT003.

\begin{theorem}[Exact coupling distance and nonisolation]
For two members $J_a,J_b$ of~\eqref{eq:coupling},
\[
 d_{\TV}(J_a,J_b)=2|a-b|.
\]
Consequently the shared-driver endpoint $a=p$ is not isolated: for every
$p\in(0,1)$ and every sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, the law
$a=p-\varepsilon$ is admitted, fully natural, has the same one-token
marginals, and lies at total-variation distance $2\varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Changing $a$ to $b$ changes the four ordered masses by
$(b-a,-(b-a),-(b-a),b-a)$. Half the $\ell^1$ norm is $2|a-b|$.
If $0<\varepsilon<\min(p,1-p)$, then
$p-\varepsilon\geq\max(0,2p-1)$, so~\eqref{eq:interval} is retained.
\end{proof}

Thus finite-variation continuity does not become robust selection. The shared
law is a disclosed proposal choice at a boundary of a continuum of equally
natural laws.

\section{Spectrum and finite-lag ambiguity}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $n$-step law]
The eigenvalues of $K_p$ are $1$ and $r=2p-1$, and
\begin{equation}
 K_p^n=\frac12
 \begin{pmatrix}
  1+r^n&1-r^n\\
  1-r^n&1+r^n
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
For every even $n$, $K_p^n=K_{1-p}^n$. For every odd $n$, the two laws differ
unless $p=1/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The constant and antisymmetric vectors diagonalize~\eqref{eq:kernel}, with
eigenvalues $1$ and $p-(1-p)=2p-1$. Equation~\eqref{eq:power} follows from
the two spectral projectors. Replacing $p$ by $1-p$ sends $r$ to $-r$.
\end{proof}

Even-lag transition evidence therefore cannot identify the proposal parameter.
Odd-lag data can distinguish the pair, but the candidate does not provide an
occurrence process, observation cadence, or source reason to privilege that
data. The theorem is a bounded identifiability result, not a claim that $p$ is
unobservable under every enrichment.

\section{Continuous-time embeddability and clock scale}

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous two-state embedding classification]
The matrix $K_p$ is the finite-positive-time transition matrix of a homogeneous
two-state continuous-time Markov chain if and only if $p>1/2$. On that branch
it admits the complement-equivariant generator
\[
 Q_\lambda=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda&\lambda\\
 \lambda&-\lambda\end{pmatrix},\qquad \lambda>0,
\]
with
\begin{equation}
 p=\frac{1+e^{-2\lambda\tau}}2,
 \qquad
 \lambda\tau=-\frac12\log(2p-1).
 \label{eq:clock}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any finite real Markov generator $Q$ and finite $\tau>0$,
$\det(e^{\tau Q})=e^{\tau\operatorname{tr}Q}>0$. But
$\det K_p=2p-1$. Hence $p<1/2$ is impossible, while $p=1/2$ is singular and
cannot be a finite matrix exponential. If $p>1/2$, direct exponentiation of
$Q_\lambda$ gives a nontrivial eigenvalue $e^{-2\lambda\tau}=2p-1$ and hence
exactly~\eqref{eq:kernel}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Rate--duration nonidentifiability]
Even on the embeddable branch, the one-step law fixes only the product
$\lambda\tau$. It selects neither a transition rate nor an elapsed duration.
\end{corollary}

The nonembeddable half of the parameter domain does not make the declared
discrete-time kernel mathematically invalid. It blocks only the overread that
the unchanged candidate already provides uniform homogeneous continuous-time
occurrence semantics. Time-inhomogeneous dynamics, hidden states, or a larger
carrier would be materially different candidates requiring their own audit.

\section{Stationarity is not occurrence}

For any measure $\mu$ on the complement orbits,
\[
 T_p\mu=p\mu+(1-p)c_*\mu.
\]
Since $1-p\neq0$, stationarity is exactly $c_*\mu=\mu$, independent of $p$.
The uniform measure on a two-token orbit is stationary for every $p$, and
arbitrary weights among distinct complement orbits remain free.

A stationary measure is not a realized token. A Markov kernel also does not
choose an initial law. Finally, a probability law does not sample itself: a
sample space, seed or random source, and realization map are additional data.
The token swap has no fixed token, so the disclosed symmetry provides no
deterministic token selector.

Total admission does not repair these omissions. Because
$\operatorname{Adm}(O)$ is the whole section fiber, it accepts every $p$ and
every coupling in~\eqref{eq:interval}; it is formally valid but
nondiscriminating.
