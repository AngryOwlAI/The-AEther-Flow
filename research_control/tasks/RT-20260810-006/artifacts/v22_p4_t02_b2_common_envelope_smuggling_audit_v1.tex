\section{Representative equivalence and normalization}

\begin{lemma}[Exact separation of the two quotient actions]
Let $r=(r_R,r_S,r_D)\in(\RR\setminus\{0\})^3$ and let
$a=(a_R,a_S,a_D)\in(\RR_{>0})^3$.  Then $a\mathbin{\cdot}r$ lies on the
same positive common-scale ray as $r$ exactly when
$a_R=a_S=a_D$.  The ordered sign orbit of $r$ is unchanged for every such
positive $a$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $a\mathbin{\cdot}r=cr$ for one $c>0$, nonvanishing of every $r_s$
implies $a_s=c$ in each sector.  The converse is immediate.  Independent
positive factors preserve every component sign, proving the second statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Rank-two response image and one projective parameter]
For the exact split candidate,
\[
 \operatorname{im}(\Resp)=
 H:=\{r\in\RR^3:r_R+r_D=2r_S\},
 \qquad \operatorname{rank}(\Resp)=2.
\]
Moreover $\ker(\Resp)=\operatorname{ann}(\operatorname{span}\{u,z\})$
has dimension two, and the positive-projective image has one independent
continuous parameter.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $a=k(u)$ and $b=k(z)$.  Then
\[
 \Resp(k)=(a+\epsilon b,a,a-\epsilon b),
\]
so every response lies in $H$.  Since $h_0(u)=1$, $h_0(z)=0$, and $z\ne0$,
the vectors $u,z$ are linearly independent.  Hence arbitrary $(a,b)$ are
realized by covectors and every point of $H$ occurs.  The image therefore has
dimension two and rank--nullity gives the kernel dimension.  On the nonzero
domain $a=p_S(k)\ne0$, and
\[
 [\Resp(k)]_+=[1+t,1,1-t]_+,
 \qquad t=\epsilon\frac{k(z)}{k(u)}
       =\frac{p_R-p_D}{2p_S}.
\]
Thus common positive projectivization leaves the one parameter $t$.
\end{proof}

Therefore $\Qray$ is legitimate formal ratio syntax only for the explicitly
fixed representatives, modulo one common scale, but its actual image has one
independent projective parameter.  The repeated RT005 claim of two independent
relative response ratios is false and requires repair.  The coarser $\Qsign$
is invariant under independent positive normalization but discards all
relative magnitude.  On the exact image it realizes six, not eight, nonzero
ordered sign cells; the alternating cells $(+,-,+)$ and $(-,+,-)$ violate
$r_R+r_D=2r_S$.

Positive rescaling is essential.  A negative factor flips one sector sign and
changes the selected component; a zero factor leaves the nonzero-response
domain and destroys invertibility.  Neither is silently quotient-repaired.

\section{Units, degree, and response type}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional homogeneity and unit boundary]
For the written equal-degree family,
$\Resp(\lambda k)=\lambda\Resp(k)$ for every $\lambda>0$, so positive
covector rescaling preserves $\Qray$.  If sector degrees differ, the response
coordinates acquire unequal powers of $\lambda$ and generally leave the
original response ray.  Positive independent unit conversions likewise change
$\Qray$ while preserving $\Qsign$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each written $p_s$ is homogeneous of degree one, which gives the common factor
$\lambda$.  Degrees $d_s$ instead give factors $\lambda^{d_s}$, common only
under special coincidences.  A positive unit conversion is componentwise
multiplication by an independent positive factor, so the preceding lemma
applies.
\end{proof}

This is not a dimensional or physical calibration result.  The three scores
share a formal real codomain in the proposal, but no empirical cross-sector
unit identification is established.  $\Qray$ ratios therefore have only the
declared fixed-representative algebraic meaning.  $\Qsign$ avoids positive-unit
dependence by discarding magnitudes.  Multiplicity, unequal degree, field
redefinition, timing, speed, and calibration remain outside both quotients.

The response type is equally explicit.  $\Resp$ is a new formal request/readout
map, not an existing P7 response space.  The names
\texttt{principal\_positive}, \texttt{principal\_zero}, and
\texttt{principal\_negative} are proposal tokens, not measurements or device
outcomes.
