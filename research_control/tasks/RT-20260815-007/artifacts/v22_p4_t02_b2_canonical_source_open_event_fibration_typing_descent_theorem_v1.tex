\section{Typed carrier}

\begin{definition}[Declared source-open category]
Let $\mathsf{OpenSrc}_4$ be the category whose objects are nonempty smooth
four-manifolds, under the standard boundaryless-manifold convention, and their
declared open submanifolds and whose arrows are smooth open embeddings.  The
empty subset is admitted as an element of every open-set lattice, but not as an
object of $\mathsf{OpenSrc}_4$.
\end{definition}

For an object $U$, write $\operatorname{Open}(U)$ for its set of topologically
open subsets, ordered by inclusion.  For an arrow $j:V\hookrightarrow U$, set
\[
  j^{*}(O)=j^{-1}(O),\qquad O\in\operatorname{Open}(U).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Canonical contravariant Open carrier]
The assignment $U\mapsto\operatorname{Open}(U)$ and
$j\mapsto j^{*}$ is a contravariant functor
\[
  \operatorname{Open}:\mathsf{OpenSrc}_4^{\mathrm{op}}
  \longrightarrow \mathsf{Poset}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Continuity of $j$ makes $j^{-1}(O)$ open.  The identity satisfies
$\operatorname{id}_{U}^{-1}(O)=O$, and for composable arrows
$W\xrightarrow{k}V\xrightarrow{j}U$ one has
$(j\circ k)^{-1}(O)=k^{-1}(j^{-1}(O))$.  Inverse image also preserves
inclusion, finite intersections, and arbitrary unions.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Category of elements]
The category $\int\!\operatorname{Open}$ has objects $(U,O)$ with
$O\in\operatorname{Open}(U)$.  An arrow over $j:V\hookrightarrow U$ from
$(V,P)$ to $(U,O)$ is admitted exactly when $P=j^{-1}(O)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Typed discrete-fibration property]
For every $j:V\hookrightarrow U$ and every $(U,O)$, there is a unique lift over
$j$ with domain $(V,j^{-1}(O))$.  Thus the projection
$\int\!\operatorname{Open}\to\mathsf{OpenSrc}_4$ is the discrete fibration
associated with the contravariant functor above.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Existence is the inverse-image construction.  The defining equality for an
arrow in the category of elements forces the domain open to equal
$j^{-1}(O)$, hence gives uniqueness.  This statement introduces no proposal
object by semantic relabeling.
\end{proof}

\section{Unique cover descent}

Let $\{j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow U\}_{i\in I}$ be a declared open cover.  Suppose
$O_i\in\operatorname{Open}(U_i)$ and, on every overlap,
\[
  O_i\cap U_i\cap U_j=O_j\cap U_i\cap U_j.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Unique open-cover descent]
There is a unique $O\in\operatorname{Open}(U)$ satisfying
$O\cap U_i=O_i$ for every $i$, namely $O=\bigcup_i O_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each $O_i$ is open in the open submanifold $U_i$ and hence open in $U$; their
union is open.  Compatibility implies that restricting the union to $U_i$
returns $O_i$.  If $O'$ has the same restrictions, the covering property gives
$O'=\bigcup_i(O'\cap U_i)=\bigcup_iO_i=O$.
\end{proof}

\section{Full-diffeomorphism natural sections}

\begin{lemma}[Point transitivity]
If $U$ is a connected boundaryless smooth manifold of positive dimension, then for any
$x,y\in U$ there exists a diffeomorphism $\phi:U\to U$ with $\phi(x)=y$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Join $x$ to $y$ by a piecewise smooth path inside the connected manifold.
Finitely many coordinate balls cover the compact path image.  In each ball a
compactly supported flow moves the current point to the next; their finite
composition is the required diffeomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Connected full-Diff trivial-section theorem]
Let $U$ be connected and boundaryless, and let $S(U)\in\operatorname{Open}(U)$ satisfy
$\phi(S(U))=S(U)$ for every $\phi\in\operatorname{Diff}(U)$.  Then
$S(U)=\varnothing$ or $S(U)=U$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $S(U)$ is nonempty, choose $x\in S(U)$.  For every $y\in U$, point
transitivity gives $\phi(x)=y$; invariance then implies $y\in S(U)$.  Hence the
nonempty case is total.
\end{proof}

Connectedness, the boundaryless convention, and the full-diffeomorphism arrow
class are load-bearing.  A disconnected object, a manifold with boundary, or a
restricted arrow class may admit proper nonempty invariant opens and is
therefore a direct falsifier of any hypothesis-free version of this theorem.
The conclusion is objectwise on each diffeomorphism-groupoid component; it does
not force different nonisomorphic components to make the same empty-versus-total
choice.

\section{Carrier does not determine occurrence}

The empty and total assignments form a fixed two-point mathematical subcarrier
$E_U=\{\varnothing,U\}$.  For every $t\in[0,1]$, define
\[
  \mu_t=(1-t)\delta_{\varnothing}+t\delta_U.
\]
Each $\mu_t$ is normalized and natural under open embeddings on this binary
subcarrier, yet $\mu_s\neq\mu_t$ whenever $s\neq t$.

\begin{theorem}[Carrier--occurrence underdetermination]
The Open carrier, its restriction maps, and its cover descent do not select a
unique normalized formal kernel, an admissibility law, a realized token, or an
occurrence generator.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The carrier data is fixed while the family $\{\mu_t\}_{t\in[0,1]}$ contains
distinct normalized kernels.  Therefore no unique kernel can be recovered from
that fixed data alone.  The $\mu_t$ are proof controls only and receive no
physical-probability, empirical-frequency, or realized-occurrence semantics.
\end{proof}

\section{Falsifiers and limits}

The theorem would fail or narrow if an admitted arrow were not continuous, if
a declared family did not cover, if local opens disagreed on overlaps, if the
object were disconnected, if naturality used less than the full diffeomorphism
group, or if a typed current-source semantic map to independently defined
proposal objects were later supplied.  Those cases do not retroactively alter
the theorem under its printed hypotheses.  They also do not prove that every
future conservative source extension is impossible.

No conclusion here constructs $g_{\mathrm{eff}}$, matter coupling, or Einstein
equations, and no Distance-to-GR row changes.
