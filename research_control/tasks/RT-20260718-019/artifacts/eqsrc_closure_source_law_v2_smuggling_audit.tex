\]
and states that \(\sim_{\mathrm{cand},v2}\) is an equivalence relation whenever
the declared family satisfies every clause of \(\Law\).

The audit asks five separate questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Does any written premise import a target atlas, target metric,
  reconstructed geometry, benchmark success, or downstream GR result?
  \item Does any registry, role, validator, approval, generated derivative,

These questions are logically independent. A conditional theorem can have a
valid proof while the language used to state its hypotheses remains
underdeclared.

\section{Audit Method}

\begin{definition}[Declaration-closure test]
Let \(L(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}})\) be the least typed language containing exactly
the domains, constants, functions, relations, and formation rules declared by
\(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}\). A candidate term is declaration-closed only if each
of its nonlogical symbols is either:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item declared in \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}\) with its complete type;
  \item generated by an explicitly declared least term algebra; or
  \item introduced by a local quantifier with its domain stated.
\end{enumerate}
A source label on an undeclared function is not a formation rule.
\end{definition}

The audit uses four findings:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \texttt{not\_detected}: the inspected target or process channel is not
  a written premise;
  \item \texttt{conditional\_pass}: the mathematical implication is valid
  once its language has a well-formed interpretation;
  \item \texttt{open\_channel}: the text intends a source-only object but does
  not close its formation or dependency rules; and
  \item \texttt{repair\_required}: a claimed closed specification uses
  undeclared or circularly admissible symbols.
\end{itemize}

\section{Accepted-Totality and Conditional-Theorem Audit}

\begin{proposition}[The repaired theorem core is valid]
For any well-formed interpretation in which the three accepted-totality
clauses hold, the relation \(\sim_{\mathrm{cand},v2}\) is an equivalence
relation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Accepted identity totality gives, for each \(A\), an accepted witness
\(\mathbf 1_A:A\to A\), so the relation is reflexive. Given an accepted
\(w:A\to B\), accepted inverse totality gives an accepted
\(w^{-1}:B\to A\), so the relation is symmetric. Given accepted
\(w:A\to B\) and \(v:B\to C\), accepted composition totality gives an accepted
\(v\circ w:A\to C\), so the relation is transitive.
\end{proof}

This proof uses exactly the repaired quantifiers. Unit, inverse,
associativity, and component congruence are useful for a coherent certificate
algebra but are not needed for the three equivalence axioms. Thus
\texttt{OBST-EQSRC-CLOSURE-TOTALITY-001} is repaired at the proposal's
statement level. That repair neither supplies a well-formed interpretation
from current ontology nor adopts the law.

\section{Closed-Grammar Audit}

\subsection{Proxy constructor escape}

The declared signature contains the set
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{src}}\) and predicates
\(\mathsf{ProxyOK}_{A,B}\), but it declares no constants or functions
\[
 p_{\mathsf{id},A},\qquad
 p_{\mathsf{inv}}(w),\qquad
 p_{\mathsf{comp}}(v,w).
\]
The accepted-totality clauses use all three. The identity symbol can be read
charitably as a locally chosen witness, although its quantifier should be made
explicit. The inverse and composition expressions are function terms whose
function symbols are neither in \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}\) nor generated by a
least proxy term algebra. Their source-side totality, codomains, and
target-independence are therefore not formally stated.

\subsection{Congruence escape}

The signature declares only the ledger congruence
\(\equiv_{\mathcal L}\). The certificate-congruence clause then requires proxy
data, negative-control outcomes, and provenance terms to agree under
``declared source congruences,'' but no proxy congruence, negative-outcome
comparison, or provenance congruence is declared. The new relation
\(\simeq_{\mathrm{src}}\) is described inside the law rather than included in
the signature with its domain and formation rule. This does not invalidate the
three-line equivalence proof, but it leaves the advertised coherent certificate
algebra underdetermined.

\subsection{Negative-control typing and dependency}

The signature says that each \(\mathsf{NegPass}_{n}\) has arguments ranging
only over the preceding source domains. Acceptance later applies it as
\(\mathsf{NegPass}_{n}(w)\), where \(w\) belongs to the certificate domain
defined after the signature and contains a provenance term from the later
least algebra. The text does not specify whether the predicate is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item a predicate on the whole certificate;
  \item a componentwise predicate with a displayed product domain; or
  \item a derived predicate defined after certificates exist.
\end{enumerate}
The current typing claim and the later application cannot both be audited
without one of those declarations.

The text also excludes target and process definitions but does not explicitly
require that \(\mathsf{LedgerOK}\), \(\mathsf{ProxyOK}\), and
\(\mathsf{NegPass}\) be primitive independent predicates or definitions whose
dependency graph precedes and excludes \(\Accept\). Consequently, an
acceptance predicate can be source-labelled yet definitionally circular.

\section{Minimal Mathematical Witnesses}

\begin{proposition}[Undeclared-term witness]
Under the declaration-closure test, the v2 law is not a sentence of
\(L(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}})\) as written.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The terms \(p_{\mathsf{inv}}(w)\) and
\(p_{\mathsf{comp}}(v,w)\) occur in required certificate tuples. Neither
function symbol is declared by \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}}\), generated by the
least provenance algebra, or introduced by a local quantifier. Hence the
terms are not terms of \(L(\Sigma_{\mathrm{src}})\). The certificate
congruence clause independently refers to undeclared component congruences.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Circular-acceptance underdetermination witness]
The present text admits a source-only reading for which candidate acceptance
is not uniquely defined.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take one well-typed certificate \(w\), one negative-control label \(n\), and
let every non-negative-control conjunct in the acceptance equation be true.
Because no dependency-order rule forbids it, read
\(\mathsf{NegPass}_{n}(w)\) as \(\Accept(w)\). The defining equation then
reduces to
\[
 \Accept(w)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Accept(w).
\]
Both truth assignments satisfy the equation. If instead the predicate is read
as \(\neg\Accept(w)\), no truth assignment satisfies it. Neither reading uses
target or process data. The witness shows a missing non-circularity condition;
it does not claim that every intended interpretation is circular.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Repairability witness]
The obstruction is not inconsistency of the accepted-totality core. Declare
the proxy constructors with typed codomains, take primitive acceptance
component predicates independent of \(\Accept\), declare the missing
congruences, and let a one-object source family accept the constructor closure
of its identity certificate. The three totality clauses then hold and the
induced one-object relation is an equivalence relation. This establishes a
bounded source-only repair path, not current-ontology derivation or adoption.
\end{remark}

These witnesses are new mathematical payload. They narrow the v2 audit to a
specific language-closure and dependency-order defect rather than repeating
the v1 accepted-totality countermodels.
